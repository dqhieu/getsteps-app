# Average Jogging Speed by Age, Sex & Fitness Level

The average jogging speed is **4 to 6 mph** (6.4 to 9.7 km/h), which works out to a pace of roughly 10 to 15 minutes per mile. Beginners typically start near 4 to 4.5 mph, while experienced joggers often settle around 6 to 7 mph.

Jogging sits deliberately between walking and running. It is fast enough to raise your heart rate into an aerobic training zone, but slow enough to hold a conversation and sustain for 30 minutes or more.

## Average Jogging Speed by Age

Age influences pace, but less than most people assume. Fitness matters more than birth year within any given decade.

| Age Group | Typical Jogging Speed | Pace per Mile |
|-----------|----------------------|---------------|
| Teens–20s | 5.5–6.0 mph | 10:00–10:55 |
| 30s | 5.0–5.5 mph | 10:55–12:00 |
| 40s | 4.5–5.5 mph | 10:55–13:20 |
| 50s | 4.5–5.0 mph | 12:00–13:20 |
| 60s | 3.5–4.5 mph | 13:20–17:10 |
| 70+ | 3.0–4.0 mph | 15:00–20:00 |

Teens and people in their twenties often jog closer to 5.5 or 6 mph, supported by higher muscle mass and recovery capacity. After 40, both men and women see a gradual decline in average speed tied to changes in strength and endurance.

The caveat matters more than the table: a trained 55-year-old routinely outpaces a sedentary 30-year-old. Use these as reference points, not targets.

## Jogging Speed by Fitness Level

| Fitness Level | Speed | Pace per Mile | What It Feels Like |
|---------------|-------|---------------|--------------------|
| Beginner | 4.0–4.5 mph | 13:20–15:00 | Can speak in full sentences |
| Recreational | 5.0–5.5 mph | 10:55–12:00 | Short sentences, steady breathing |
| Experienced | 6.0–7.0 mph | 8:35–10:00 | A few words at a time |
| Competitive | 7.0+ mph | under 8:35 | Conversation not sustainable |

A safe jogging speed for beginners falls between 4 and 4.5 mph. That range lets the heart and connective tissue adapt while keeping injury risk low, which is the main reason to resist starting faster.

## Jogging vs Walking vs Running

The boundaries are fuzzy, but effort and cadence separate them more reliably than speed alone.

| Activity | Typical Speed | Pace per Mile |
|----------|--------------|---------------|
| Casual walk | 2.5–3.0 mph | 20:00–24:00 |
| Brisk walk | 3.5–4.0 mph | 15:00–17:10 |
| Jogging | 4.0–6.0 mph | 10:00–15:00 |
| Running | 6.0–8.0 mph | 7:30–10:00 |
| Sprinting | 12.0+ mph | under 5:00 |

Notice the overlap: a brisk 4 mph walk and a slow 4 mph jog cover ground at the same rate. The difference is gait. Jogging includes a flight phase where both feet leave the ground, which raises impact forces and calorie burn even at identical speed.

## How Many Steps Is Jogging?

Jogging cadence is higher than walking cadence, but stride is longer, so steps per mile actually fall.

| Activity | Cadence | Steps per Mile |
|----------|---------|----------------|
| Walking (3 mph) | ~110 spm | ~2,100 |
| Jogging (5 mph) | ~160 spm | ~1,700 |
| Running (7 mph) | ~175 spm | ~1,500 |

A 30-minute jog at 5 mph covers 2.5 miles and produces roughly **4,250 steps**. The same 30 minutes walking at 3 mph covers 1.5 miles and produces about 3,300 steps. Jogging wins on both distance and step count, though the gap in steps is smaller than most people expect.

## How to Find Your Own Jogging Pace

**Use the talk test.** True jogging pace is one where you can speak in short sentences but not sing. If you cannot get a sentence out, you are running, not jogging, and you will not sustain it.

**Try 30 minutes at a steady effort.** Track the distance, then divide to get your pace. Repeat weekly. Improvement shows up as more distance at the same perceived effort, not as a faster number you forced.

**Watch cadence, not just speed.** Most efficient joggers land near 160 to 180 steps per minute. Raising cadence slightly while keeping speed constant usually reduces impact and overstriding.

**Build volume before pace.** Injury risk rises fastest when runners add speed and distance in the same week. Increase weekly mileage by no more than 10%, and hold pace steady while you do.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is 5 mph a good jogging speed?

Yes. 5 mph is a 12-minute mile and sits squarely in the recreational jogging range. It is a sustainable aerobic pace for most reasonably fit adults and a sensible target for someone progressing beyond a beginner pace.

### What is the average jogging speed for a beginner?

Beginners typically jog at 4 to 4.5 mph, or a 13:20 to 15:00 mile. Starting here rather than faster lets tendons and ligaments adapt alongside cardiovascular fitness, which is where most early injuries come from.

### Is jogging 3 mph possible?

3 mph is generally too slow to jog efficiently. Most people find the gait awkward below about 4 mph and are better served by brisk walking, which at 3.5 to 4 mph delivers comparable aerobic benefit with less joint impact.

### How fast should a 60-year-old jog?

Around 3.5 to 4.5 mph for most active 60-year-olds. Fitness history matters far more than age: lifelong runners frequently hold 5.5 mph or better into their sixties. Effort level is the better guide than any age-based table.

### Does jogging burn more calories than walking?

Yes, roughly 2.5 times per minute. A 155 lb person burns about 305 calories jogging for 30 minutes at 5 mph versus about 116 calories walking at 3 mph. Per mile the gap narrows considerably, because walking that mile simply takes longer.

### How long does it take to jog a mile?

At the 4 to 6 mph average range, one mile takes 10 to 15 minutes. Beginners often start nearer 15 minutes, while experienced joggers commonly run 8:30 to 10:00 miles.
