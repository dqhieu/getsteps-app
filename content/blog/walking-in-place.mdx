# Walking in Place: Calories, Steps, and a 20-Min Plan

**Walking in place burns calories when your feet actually leave the floor.** In a lab comparison, continuous stepping cost about 258 kcal per hour in the average adult they measured, versus about 304 kcal per hour walking on a treadmill at 3.0 mph, versus about 81 kcal per hour sitting. That is roughly 85% of the treadmill walk. Shuffling in place barely raises energy use. The lift is what makes the bout real work.

This page stays on marching and stepping in one spot: bad weather, a small room, or a show you would otherwise sit through. Longer indoor routines are separate: a pad, treadmill, mall, or stairs in the [indoor walking workout](/blog/indoor-walking-workout), and a no-equipment living-room progression in the [walking at home workout](/blog/walking-at-home-workout).

## Walking in Place Calories vs Sitting and a 3 mph Walk

Steeves and colleagues published a lab comparison in *Medicine & Science in Sports & Exercise* — the TV-commercial stepping study. They measured continuous stepping in place, treadmill walking at 3.0 mph, and sitting.

| Activity | About kcal per hour |
|----------|---------------------|
| Sitting | 81 |
| Stepping in place, continuous, feet leaving the floor | 258 |
| Treadmill walk at 3.0 mph | 304 |

Those figures are for their average adult. Stepping landed at roughly **85%** of the treadmill walk.

Participants told to step during TV commercials logged about **2,100 steps in about 25 minutes**. Commercial-break stepping is enough to matter for a daily step total. One lab bout does not establish a fat-loss amount.

Outdoor walking calories by weight and speed are in [calories burned walking 30 minutes](/blog/calories-burned-walking-30-minutes). The [Walking Calories Calculator](/tools/walking-calories-calculator) estimates a normal walk. The [Steps to Calories Calculator](/tools/steps-to-calories-calculator) converts a step count. Both describe walking. The direct rate for continuous stepping in place remains about 258 kcal per hour.

Arithmetic from that hourly rate, for their average adult: 20 minutes is 258 × 20/60, about **86 kcal**. Thirty minutes is 258 × 30/60, about **129 kcal**.

## Walking in Place Form: Cadence and Foot Height

Two cues come from that line of research.

**Cadence.** A moderate rate is about **100–120 steps per minute**. Count each footfall as one step, the way a pedometer does. To check the rate, count footfalls for 15 seconds and multiply by 4. Twenty-five footfalls is 100 per minute. Thirty is 120. The higher end is harder to hold once the feet are actually lifting.

**Foot height.** Lift the feet several inches, about **6–8 inches**, rather than scraping the floor. A short block of higher knees makes that clearance obvious. A shuffle can look busy and still sit close to standing in energy cost.

Swing the arms with the step. Higher knees can be short blocks inside a 20-minute bout. The foot still has to leave the floor on the steps you intend to count. If balance is shaky, rest one hand on a chair back so you can keep that lift.

## A 20-Minute Walking in Place Plan

This session is built from the cadence and foot-height cues. The 2,100-step finding came from stepping during commercials, a different pattern from these 20 continuous minutes. Before you start, put the phone in a pocket or wear the watch on your wrist. A phone on a desk will not count the steps. A watch arm braced on a hip or a chair records fewer of them.

| Clock | Block |
|-------|--------|
| 0:00–3:00 | Easy stepping. Feet leave the floor, with a smaller lift. |
| 3:00–5:00 | Higher knees, about 6–8 inches, toward 110–120 steps per minute. |
| 5:00–6:00 | Easy stepping. |
| 6:00–9:00 | Regular stepping, about 100–110 steps per minute, arms swinging. |
| 9:00–10:00 | Easy stepping. |
| 10:00–12:00 | Higher knees. Shorten the block if the feet start to scrape. |
| 12:00–13:00 | Easy stepping. |
| 13:00–16:00 | Regular stepping. Feet still leave the floor. |
| 16:00–17:00 | Easy stepping. |
| 17:00–20:00 | Cool-down. Smaller steps, easy breathing. |

Three minutes easy, then sets of higher knees and regular stepping, one easy minute between the work blocks, then a three-minute cool-down. Stay stepping on the easy minutes. Standing still adds no steps. If you are short on time, stop at 10:00, after the first regular block.

## Step Totals From Walking in Place

This block is arithmetic.

At **110 steps per minute**, with every minute spent stepping:

- **20 minutes** × 110 = **about 2,200 steps**
- **30 minutes** × 110 = **about 3,300 steps**

The plan mixes easy stepping and higher knees, so a live total will sit around 2,200. Higher knees often slow the cadence even when effort goes up. A walk that travels uses a longer stride, so the same half hour often produces a different count. See [how many steps in 30 minutes of walking](/blog/how-many-steps-in-30-minutes-walking). Other activities can be translated with the [Activity to Steps Converter](/tools/activity-to-steps-converter). Distance stays at zero because you never leave the spot.

## Will a Tracker Count Walking in Place?

A phone on a desk will not count these steps. A phone in a pocket can. An Apple Watch on the wrist can. If the numbers look low, the arm or the phone is not moving with the step.

- The phone is on a table, a counter, or the couch.
- The watch arm is braced, so the wrist stays quiet.
- The steps are a shuffle, too small to register.
- A loose pocket lets the phone swing less than the leg.

Pocket the phone, or swing the watch arm with the march, and repeat one minute.

## What Walking in Place Does Not Do

**It does not train balance on uneven ground.** The floor is flat and the path never turns. Use a chair if you are unsteady, and practice curbs and trails somewhere else.

**The stride is smaller than outdoor walking.** You never travel, so the long push-off of a real step is cut short. The bout still counts for calories and steps. It is a weak rehearsal for a long walk.

**It is a poor sole plan if the goal is a long-distance walk.** Time on your feet, over ground, is what prepares that goal. Marching in place can hold a streak on a storm day. For a longer indoor session, use the [indoor walking workout](/blog/indoor-walking-workout) or the [walking at home workout](/blog/walking-at-home-workout).

**It is a good bad-weather tool and a good TV-time tool for breaking up sitting.** Twenty focused minutes, or commercials across an evening, move the daily total. Short bouts through the day match the idea in [exercise snacks](/blog/exercise-snacks).

Body-fat change depends on the rest of the week, on food, and on time. One stepping study is not a fat-loss prescription.

## Common Questions

### Does walking in place burn calories?

Yes, when the feet leave the floor. In the Steeves comparison, continuous stepping was about 258 kcal per hour, versus about 304 kcal per hour on a treadmill at 3.0 mph and about 81 kcal per hour sitting. That is roughly 85% of the treadmill walk, in their average adult. Shuffling barely raises energy use. Twenty minutes at that rate is about 86 kcal by arithmetic.

### Does walking in place count as steps?

It can. Stepping during TV commercials produced about 2,100 steps in about 25 minutes. At 110 steps per minute, arithmetic gives about 2,200 steps in 20 minutes and about 3,300 in 30 minutes. A phone in a pocket or a watch on a moving wrist can record them. A phone on a desk stays at zero.

### Is walking in place enough on its own?

It is enough to break up sitting and to add a real piece of the daily total. It does not train balance on uneven ground, the stride is smaller than outdoor walking, and it is a poor sole plan for a long-distance goal. Use it for weather and TV time, and add a longer walk when distance is the point.

### Why did my step count stay low?

The device did not move with the step, or the feet never really lifted. A phone on a desk will not count walking in place. A phone in a pocket or an Apple Watch on the wrist can. Swing the arm, pocket the phone, and lift about 6–8 inches at roughly 100–120 steps per minute.

## The Bottom Line

Walking in place is worth doing on days when the alternative is the couch. Lift the feet several inches at about 100–120 steps per minute and you are near the 258 kcal per hour from that lab comparison, close to a 3 mph treadmill walk. Twenty minutes at 110 steps per minute is about 2,200 steps by arithmetic. Pocket the phone or wear the watch. When the goal is a long walk, go out and walk.

**This article is general information, not medical advice.** Talk to a clinician first if you have chest pain, dizziness, or a condition that makes unaccustomed exercise risky.
