# Is 9,000 Steps a Day Good?

**Yes — 9,000 steps a day is good, and it sits right in the band where modern step research piles up.** A 2023 JACC meta-analysis of 111,309 people found the greatest reduction in death from any cause at about **8,763 steps** versus a 2,000-step baseline. A UK Biobank analysis in the *British Journal of Sports Medicine* put the lowest mortality around **9,000–10,500 steps a day**, and that nadir held whether people sat a little or a lot. Northwestern work in older adults tied **6,000–9,000 steps** to a 40–50% lower risk of cardiovascular disease versus 2,000.

So if you are asking **is 9000 steps a day good**, you are not splitting a hair between 8,000 and 10,000. You are in the zone those papers actually measured. Nine thousand is a little more buffer than 8,000, a little less grind than 10,000, and for many adults under 60 it is the first number that fully covers Paluch's 8,000–10,000 plateau.

Below: what 9,000 steps looks like in miles and calories, how it compares with 7,000 / 8,000 / 10,000, and who should aim here versus stopping lower.

## What Does 9,000 Steps a Day Actually Look Like?

For an average adult, **9,000 steps a day** works out to roughly **4.0 to 4.5 miles** (6.4–7.2 km), takes around **85 to 95 minutes** of cumulative walking, and burns somewhere around **360 to 450 calories** for a 150 lb person.

| Metric | 9,000 Steps (Average Adult) |
|--------|-----------------------------|
| Distance | ~4.0–4.5 miles (6.4–7.2 km) |
| Walking time (moderate pace) | ~85–95 minutes |
| Calories burned (150 lb person) | ~360–450 cal |
| vs. US average (3,000–4,000) | ~2.5x more active |
| vs. 8,000 steps | ~+0.4–0.5 miles (~10 min) |

## What the Research Says About 9,000 Steps a Day

**JACC 2023 (Stens, Ortega, Eijsvogels):** mortality benefit started ~2,517 steps; CVD ~2,735; **optimum ~8,763** (aHR 0.40 vs 2,000) and **~7,126** for new CVD. Nine thousand is a rounding error above that optimum, not a different sport.

**Paluch 2022 (Lancet Public Health):** curve flattened ~6,000–8,000 for age 60+ and ~8,000–10,000 under 60. For younger adults, **9,000 is inside the plateau**, not past it.

**Lancet Public Health 2025 (Ding):** 7,000 already captured most of the 10,000-step benefit; extra steps still helped some outcomes a little. Nine thousand is a reasonable "I have 20 more minutes" upgrade from 7,000 without pretending 10,000 is magic. Full 7,000 argument: [is 7,000 steps a day enough](/blog/is-7000-steps-a-day-enough).

**BJSM / UK Biobank (sedentary time):** lowest death risk **9,000–10,500 steps**, independent of sitting time. Minimal useful dose ~4,000–4,500. That paper is why 9,000 is a particularly clean public-facing target if you sit at a desk all day.

**Northwestern (older adults, CVD):** 6,000–9,000 vs 2,000 was associated with 40–50% lower cardiovascular disease. For 60+, 9,000 is the top of a very good range, not a requirement.

## Is 9,000 Steps a Day Good Compared to 8,000 and 10,000?

| Metric | 8,000 Steps | 9,000 Steps | 10,000 Steps |
|--------|-------------|-------------|--------------|
| Distance (avg) | ~3.5–4.0 mi | ~4.0–4.5 mi | ~4.4–5.0 mi |
| Time (moderate) | ~75–85 min | ~85–95 min | ~95–105 min |
| Calories (150 lb) | ~320–400 cal | ~360–450 cal | ~400–500 cal |
| Closest evidence | JACC optimum ~8,763 | BJSM nadir 9–10.5k | Tracker default |
| Age fit | Strong default all ages | Best under-60 plateau | Extra headroom |
| Adherence | High | High | Needs a dedicated hour more often |

The jump from 8,000 to 9,000 is about **10 minutes**. The jump from 9,000 to 10,000 is another **10–15 minutes** for a thin extra slice of benefit. If 8,000 is easy, 9,000 is the evidence-aligned next step. If 9,000 is already a stretch, stay there. 10,000 remains a fine goal; it is not a pass/fail line. ## Is 9,000 Steps a Day Good for Weight Loss?

It helps more than 7,000–8,000. A 150 lb adult burns roughly 360–450 calories at 9,000 steps — about **2,500–3,150 a week**, in the neighborhood of 0.7–0.9 lb of fat if diet holds. Appetite still rises. Hold 9,000 as a **floor** if fat loss is the job, walk part of it briskly, and keep food honest.

## Is 9,000 Steps a Day Good for Every Age?

**Under 60:** Paluch kept seeing benefit into 8,000–10,000. Nine thousand is a sweet default.

**60 and older:** Most longevity benefit is in by 6,000–8,000. Nine thousand is excellent if joints allow; it is not mandatory. CVD data up to 9,000 is encouraging.

**Coming from 4,000–5,000:** Do not jump. Add 500–1,000 steps a week. Age bands: [recommended steps per day by age](/blog/recommended-steps-per-day-by-age).

## How to Hit 9,000 Steps a Day

Most people bank 3,000–5,000 without trying. The gap to 9,000 is about **4,000–6,000 extra steps** — 40–55 minutes, ideally split.

- **One 30–40 minute walk** (~3,200–4,500 steps).
- **Walking calls** for one meeting.
- **Post-meal 15 minutes** for glucose and extra cadence.
- **One transit stop early.**

Track the real total in the [Steps app](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/steps-workout-pedometer/id6746096378). "I walk a lot" and 9,000 are not the same thing until a sensor says so.

## Common Questions

### Is 9,000 steps a day good for your health?

Yes. It sits next to the JACC ~8,763-step optimum and inside the BJSM 9,000–10,500 lowest-mortality band.

### How many miles is 9,000 steps?

About **4.0–4.5 miles** (6.4–7.2 km) for the average adult. See [how many miles is 9,000 steps](/blog/how-many-miles-is-9000-steps).

### How long does it take to walk 9,000 steps?

Roughly **85–95 minutes** cumulative at a moderate pace, less if part of the day is brisk.

### How many calories does 9,000 steps burn?

About **360–450 for a 150 lb person**, more if you are heavier or walking faster.

### Is 9,000 steps better than 10,000?

For longevity, nearly the same. Ten thousand adds time and a modest extra calorie burn. Nine thousand is the better adherence target for most people.

### Is 9,000 steps a day good for seniors?

Yes if it feels sustainable. For many people 60+, 7,000–8,000 already captures most of the mortality curve; 9,000 is a bonus, not a test.
