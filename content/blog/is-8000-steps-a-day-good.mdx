# Is 8,000 Steps a Day Good?

**Yes — 8,000 steps a day is good, and for most adults it sits right at the dose researchers now call optimal.** A 2023 meta-analysis in the *Journal of the American College of Cardiology*, covering 111,309 people across 12 studies, found the greatest reduction in death from any cause at about **8,763 steps a day** — a 60% lower risk compared with 2,000 steps. Cardiovascular benefit peaked even earlier, around **7,126 steps**. University of Granada researchers, who co-led the work, described 8,000 steps as the first scientifically grounded target for cutting premature death.

So if you're asking is 8000 steps a day good, the short answer is that it is not a consolation prize for missing 10,000. It is where the longevity curve actually flattens.

Below: what 8,000 steps a day looks like in miles and calories, how 8,000 compares with 7,000 and 10,000, and who should aim higher or lower.

## What Does 8,000 Steps a Day Actually Look Like?

For an average adult, **8,000 steps a day** works out to roughly **3.5 to 4 miles** (about 5.6–6.4 km), takes around **75 to 85 minutes** of cumulative walking, and burns somewhere around **320 to 400 calories** for a 150 lb person.

| Metric | 8,000 Steps (Average Adult) |
|--------|------------------------------|
| Distance | ~3.5–4.0 miles (5.6–6.4 km) |
| Walking time (moderate pace) | ~75–85 minutes |
| Calories burned (150 lb person) | ~320–400 cal |
| vs. US average (3,000–4,000) | ~2–2.5x more active |

That 75–85 minutes is **cumulative**, not a lunch-hour block. Commuting, errands, pacing during calls and a 20-minute evening loop all count.

## What the Research Says About 8,000 Steps a Day

The JACC 2023 paper — Stens, Ortega, Eijsvogels and colleagues — is the cleanest answer to whether 8,000 steps a day is good for living longer. Researchers pooled 12 cohorts that used pedometers or accelerometers, not self-reported activity. Mean age was about 62. Compared with a sedentary baseline of 2,000 steps a day:

- Mortality risk started falling at just **2,517 steps**
- Cardiovascular events started falling at **2,735 steps**
- The **optimal dose** — maximum risk reduction at the least extra effort — landed at **8,763 steps** for all-cause death (adjusted hazard ratio 0.40) and **7,126 steps** for new cardiovascular disease (aHR 0.49)
- Beyond those points, more steps were not worse. The extra protection was simply small

In plain language: most of the longevity benefit of walking is in the bank by the time you reach 8,000 steps a day. Granada's summary called this the first scientific proof that roughly 8,000 steps — about 6.4 km — is the number that meaningfully cuts premature death.

Two extras from the same analysis matter in daily life. **Pace still counts:** moving from a low to a moderate or high step cadence was independently linked to lower mortality, even after accounting for total steps. **More is not harmful:** the authors were explicit that 16,000 steps a day showed no added risk. The curve just stops rewarding you as steeply.

A 2022 *Lancet Public Health* meta-analysis led by Amanda Paluch (15 cohorts, nearly 47,500 adults) found the same shape with an age split: the mortality curve plateaued around **6,000–8,000 steps for people 60 and older**, and around **8,000–10,000 for younger adults**. That is why 8,000 steps a day is such a useful default — it sits at the top of the older-adult plateau and at the floor of the younger-adult plateau.

A later 2025 Lancet review found that **7,000 steps already captures nearly all of the 10,000-step benefit**. We cover that paper in [is 7,000 steps a day enough](/blog/is-7000-steps-a-day-enough). Read together: 7,000 is enough; 8,000 is the number closest to the measured optimum.

None of this replaces medical advice. These are observational associations, not proof that steps alone cause the outcome. The consistency across independent analyses is still the strongest signal we have.

## Is 8,000 Steps a Day Good Compared to 7,000 and 10,000?

This is the comparison most people actually need.

| Metric | 7,000 Steps | 8,000 Steps | 10,000 Steps |
|--------|-------------|-------------|--------------|
| Distance (avg) | ~3.1–3.5 mi | ~3.5–4.0 mi | ~4.4–5.0 mi |
| Walking time (moderate) | ~65–75 min | ~75–85 min | ~95–105 min |
| Calories (150 lb) | ~280–350 cal | ~320–400 cal | ~400–500 cal |
| Closest evidence | Lancet 2025: ~47% lower mortality vs 2,000 | JACC 2023: optimal dose ~8,763 | Classic tracker default |
| Age fit (Paluch) | Strong for 60+ | Sweet spot across ages | Extra headroom if under 60 |
| Adherence | High | High | Lower — often needs a dedicated hour |
| Best for | Longevity on a busy schedule | Longevity + a modest extra buffer | Weight loss, higher fitness |

The honest summary: **7,000 is enough, 8,000 is optimal, 10,000 is slightly better and a lot more work.** The jump from 7,000 to 8,000 is about 10–15 extra minutes. The jump from 8,000 to 10,000 is another 20–25 minutes for a thin slice of additional mortality benefit.

If 7,000 already feels like a stretch, stay there. If 7,000 is comfortable, 8,000 steps a day is the most evidence-aligned next step. 10,000 remains a fine stretch goal; the [benefits of 10,000 steps a day](/blog/10000-steps-a-day-benefits) are real. It just isn't a pass/fail line. For context on where these numbers sit on an activity scale, see [how many steps a day is considered active](/blog/how-many-steps-a-day-is-considered-active).

## Is 8,000 Steps a Day Good for Every Age?

Age changes the target more than fitness culture admits.

**If you are 60 or older:** Paluch's analysis found most of the longevity benefit arrives by 6,000–8,000 steps. Hitting 8,000 steps a day is excellent; pushing past it for mortality alone has limited extra payoff. If joints or recovery make 8,000 unrealistic, [6,000 steps a day is still good](/blog/is-6000-steps-a-day-good) — especially as a next milestone from a sedentary baseline.

**If you are under 60:** The same analysis kept seeing benefit up to 8,000–10,000. 8,000 is the floor of that plateau, not the ceiling. If you have the time, 9,000–10,000 is a reasonable stretch.

**If you are rebuilding from very low activity:** The JACC curve is steepest in the first extra thousands of steps. Going from 3,000 to 6,000 does more for risk than going from 8,000 to 11,000. Ramp by 500–1,000 steps a week.

## Is 8,000 Steps a Day Good for Weight Loss?

It helps. It is rarely enough on its own.

Walking 8,000 steps a day burns roughly 320–400 calories for a 150 lb adult — about 2,200–2,800 calories a week. Appetite often rises with activity, so the scale moves more slowly than the arithmetic suggests.

If fat loss is the main goal, hold 8,000 as the floor rather than the ceiling, walk briskly for part of the total (cadence independently mattered in the JACC data), and pair walking with a modest calorie deficit. Nutrition remains the larger lever.

## How to Hit 8,000 Steps a Day

Most people already bank 3,000–4,000 steps through ordinary movement. Closing the gap to 8,000 means finding roughly 4,000 extra steps — about 35–40 minutes of walking, which you can split.

- **A 25–30 minute walk after a meal** — around 2,500–3,000 steps, and it blunts the post-meal glucose spike.
- **Take calls on your feet.** A 30-minute walking call is often 3,000 steps by itself.
- **Park farther or get off one stop early.** 500–800 steps each way.
- **Set an hourly two-minute lap.** Eight laps is roughly 2,000 steps.

Ramp gradually. A goal you hit six days a week beats a heroic Saturday and a silent Thursday.

## Common Questions

### Is 8,000 steps a day good for your health?

Yes. The 2023 JACC meta-analysis of 111,309 adults found the optimal dose for all-cause mortality at about 8,763 steps a day — a 60% lower risk versus 2,000 steps — with cardiovascular benefit peaking around 7,126 steps.

### How many miles is 8,000 steps?

About 3.5 to 4 miles (5.6 to 6.4 km) for the average adult. Height and stride change the number; see [how many miles is 8,000 steps](/blog/how-many-miles-is-8000-steps).

### How long does it take to walk 8,000 steps?

Roughly 75 to 85 minutes of cumulative walking at a moderate pace, or closer to 70 minutes at a brisk 3.5 mph. Most people accumulate it across the day.

### How many calories does 8,000 steps burn?

Approximately 320 to 400 calories for a 150 lb person, and 300 to 450 for most adults depending on weight and pace.

### Is 8,000 steps better than 7,000 or 10,000?

Slightly better than 7,000 and nearly as good as 10,000 for longevity. 7,000 already captures most of the benefit; 8,000 sits closest to the JACC optimum; 10,000 adds a little more at a higher time cost. Full 7,000-step case: [is 7,000 steps a day enough](/blog/is-7000-steps-a-day-enough).

### Is 8,000 steps a day good for seniors?

Yes. For adults 60 and older, Paluch's research found the longevity curve flattening around 6,000–8,000 steps, so 8,000 sits at the top of the ideal range.

### Is 8,000 steps a day enough to lose weight?

It supports fat loss but usually isn't sufficient alone. Use it as a reliable daily burn and pair it with food intake if the scale is the goal.
