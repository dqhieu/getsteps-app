# Indoor Walking Workout: Pad, Treadmill, Mall & Stairs

**An indoor walking workout is any structured walk you do under a roof — on a walking pad, a treadmill, through a mall, up stairs, or in a small room with no equipment.** In 20 to 45 minutes you can log roughly 1,600 to 6,000 steps and burn about 70 to 280 calories (for a 155 lb person), depending on speed, incline, and whether you add stairs.

This guide covers every indoor option in one place: how they compare, three ready-to-follow workouts (20, 30, and 45 minutes) with step and calorie estimates, and how to track indoor steps when GPS cannot help. If you only need a living-room, no-equipment plan, use our [walking at home workout](/blog/walking-at-home-workout) instead of repeating it here.

## Indoor Walking Workout Options: Pad, Treadmill, Mall, and Stairs

All five count as an indoor walking workout. They trade intensity, convenience, and cost.

| Option | Best for | Typical pace | Space / cost | Intensity |
|--------|----------|--------------|--------------|-----------|
| Walking pad / desk | Workday steps | 1.5–3.0 mph | Under a desk; $150–$500 | Easy–moderate |
| Treadmill | Controlled intervals | 3.0–4.2 mph + incline | Home gym or gym | Moderate–hard |
| Mall walking | Weather-proof distance | 3.0–3.5 mph | Free public space | Moderate |
| Stairs | Short, high-effort bursts | N/A (flights) | Building or mall | Hard, low-impact |
| No equipment | Zero gear, tiny space | March / room loops | Living room | Easy–brisk |

**Walking pad.** A slim under-desk treadmill built for typing-friendly speed, not sprints. At 1.5–2.0 mph you add thousands of steps during calls and reading; at 2.5–3.0 mph it becomes a real session. See [walking pad benefits](/blog/walking-pad-benefits) for calories per hour, and [walking pad vs treadmill](/blog/walking-pad-vs-treadmill) if you are choosing hardware.

**Treadmill.** Best when you want exact speed and incline. Intervals and a 1–6% grade raise heart rate without running. Full calorie tables by speed and incline live in [calories burned on a treadmill](/blog/calories-burned-on-treadmill).

**Mall walking.** Climate control, even floors, restrooms, and distance you can measure in wings or laps. Morning mall-walker groups exist in many cities for a reason: you can hold a brisk pace for 45 minutes without weather, traffic, or dark streets.

**Stairs.** Two or three flights mixed into a mall or office walk spike effort the way a treadmill incline does. Take them one step at a time, use the rail if you need it, and walk the next hallway as recovery.

**No equipment.** Marching in place, room loops, and simple directional steps still count. We keep that plan on its own page so this post stays about venues and timed sessions — jump to the [28-day walking at home workout](/blog/walking-at-home-workout) when you have zero machines and a small floor.

CDC guidelines count indoor walking toward 150 minutes a week of moderate activity. At the same pace, indoor and outdoor walking burn similar calories.

## Three Indoor Walking Workout Plans (20, 30, and 45 Minutes)

Use the [walking time calculator](/tools/walking-time-calculator) if you need to fit a session into a lunch break. Estimates below are for a **155 lb (70 kg) adult**. Lighter bodies land toward the low end; 190–220 lb toward the high end. Personalize with the [walking calories calculator](/tools/walking-calories-calculator).

### 1. 20-Minute Walking Pad (or Desk) Workout

A focused pad session — not typing. If your pad caps at 4 mph, stay in a fast walk; do not jog on a rail-less belt.

| Time | Speed | Focus |
|------|-------|--------|
| 0:00–3:00 | 1.8–2.0 mph | Easy warm-up; tall posture, arms swinging |
| 3:00–8:00 | 2.5 mph | Steady walk; look forward, not at your feet |
| 8:00–14:00 | 2.8–3.0 mph | Brisk block; shorten stride if the belt feels short |
| 14:00–17:00 | 2.2 mph | Recover; shake out shoulders |
| 17:00–20:00 | 2.6 mph, then 1.8 mph last minute | Finish + cool-down |

**Estimated steps:** 1,600–2,000 (about 2,000 steps per mile at 2.5–3.0 mph).  
**Estimated calories:** 75–110 (about 85–95 at 155 lb).

Desk bonus: keep the pad at 1.5–2.0 mph for another hour of work and you add ~3,000–4,000 easy steps without a second workout. That volume is the pad’s real job; this 20-minute block is the quality session on top.

### 2. 30-Minute Treadmill Interval Workout

Incline does the hard work so you never need to run. This is a distinct interval block — for more 30-minute templates see our [30-minute walking workout](/blog/30-minute-walking-workout), and for shorter hard/easy bursts see [HIIT walking workout](/blog/hiit-walking-workout).

| Time | Speed | Incline | Effort |
|------|-------|---------|--------|
| 0:00–5:00 | 2.8 mph | 1% | Warm-up |
| 5:00–8:00 | 3.4 mph | 2% | Brisk cruise |
| 8:00–9:00 | 3.8 mph | 4% | Push |
| 9:00–10:00 | 3.0 mph | 1% | Recover |
| 10:00–25:00 | Repeat the 3 min / 1 min / 1 min block three more times (4 pushes total) | 2% → 4–5% on pushes | Moderate–hard |
| 25:00–30:00 | 2.6–2.8 mph | 0–1% | Cool-down |

On the last two pushes, raise incline to 5% if 4% feels easy. Keep a power-walk, not a shuffle.

**Estimated steps:** 3,200–4,000.  
**Estimated calories:** 160–220 (about 175–195 at 155 lb with the inclines). Flat 3.5 mph walking is closer to 155 calories in 30 minutes; the grade is why this lands higher.

### 3. 45-Minute Mall Walk With Stairs

Treat the mall like a track. Pick two landmarks (food court and far entrance) as lap ends. Add stairs only if your joints feel good that day.

| Time | Segment | How it should feel |
|------|---------|-------------------|
| 0:00–5:00 | Easy lap, window-shopping pace | You can chat in full sentences |
| 5:00–15:00 | Brisk outer loop | Slightly breathless; 3.0–3.5 mph effort |
| 15:00–18:00 | Stairs: up one to two flights, walk the next level, down a different stairwell | Hard but controlled |
| 18:00–30:00 | Brisk hallway walking | Back to conversational-brisk |
| 30:00–33:00 | Second stair burst (or a parking-garage ramp if stairs are crowded) | Hard |
| 33:00–42:00 | Steady brisk laps | Moderate |
| 42:00–45:00 | Easy cool-down toward the exit | Easy |

No mall nearby? Use a long indoor corridor, airport terminal, or big-box store during off hours. Skip stairs if you have knee or balance issues; replace those six minutes with a faster hallway pace.

**Estimated steps:** 4,500–6,000 (stairs add a few hundred extra).  
**Estimated calories:** 200–280 (about 220–250 at 155 lb with two stair bursts).

## Calories and Steps at a Glance

| Workout | Time | Steps | Calories (155 lb) | Calories (200 lb) |
|---------|------|-------|-------------------|-------------------|
| Pad / desk session | 20 min | 1,600–2,000 | 85–95 | 110–125 |
| Treadmill intervals | 30 min | 3,200–4,000 | 175–195 | 225–250 |
| Mall + stairs | 45 min | 4,500–6,000 | 220–250 | 280–320 |

One of each per week is about **9,000–12,000 workout steps** and **480–540 calories** for a 155 lb person, before daily living steps. Set the rest of your target with the [daily step goal calculator](/tools/daily-step-goal-calculator). A mix that sticks: pad on busy weekdays, treadmill intervals twice a week, mall or stairs on the weekend.

## How to Track an Indoor Walking Workout

Indoor walking is where GPS fails. A mall roof, a basement treadmill, and a walking pad under a desk all look like “you did not go anywhere” to a satellite. Step count has to come from **motion sensors** (accelerometer and gyroscope) on your iPhone or Apple Watch.

[Steps: Workout & Pedometer](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/steps-workout-pedometer/id6746096378) reads those sensors, so pad, treadmill, mall, and stair sessions still count. Wear the watch or keep the phone on your body (pocket or armband), not on the desk beside the pad.

Walking-pad caveat: if the phone sits on the desk, you will undercount. Wear the watch or pocket the phone. If a 30-minute treadmill walk adds fewer than 2,000 steps, the device was probably not on you.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is an indoor walking workout as good as walking outside?

For heart health and calorie burn at the same pace, **yes**. You miss outdoor variety, sunlight, and terrain. You gain consistency: rain, heat, ice, and dark evenings stop being reasons to skip.

### How many calories does an indoor walking workout burn?

A 155 lb person burns roughly **85–95 calories in 20 minutes** on a pad, **175–195 in 30 minutes** of treadmill intervals, and **220–250 in 45 minutes** of mall walking with stairs. Weight, speed, and incline move those numbers. Use the [walking calories calculator](/tools/walking-calories-calculator) for your stats.

### Can I do an indoor walking workout without a treadmill?

**Yes.** Mall walking, stairs, a walking pad, or a no-equipment living-room session all work. For the equipment-free plan, use the [walking at home workout](/blog/walking-at-home-workout).

### How many steps is a 30-minute indoor walking workout?

About **3,200–4,000 steps** on a treadmill at a brisk interval pace. A slower pad session in the same 30 minutes is closer to 2,400–3,000 steps. Cadence (steps per minute) matters more than the roof over your head.

### Is mall walking actually a workout?

**Yes**, if you walk with purpose. Window-shopping is light activity. A 45-minute brisk loop is moderate cardio — the same category as an outdoor fitness walk. Stairs turn it into a mixed-intensity session.

### Walking pad or treadmill — which should I buy?

A **pad** wins for all-day desk steps and small apartments. A **treadmill** wins for incline, faster walking, and interval workouts like the 30-minute plan above. Details: [walking pad vs treadmill](/blog/walking-pad-vs-treadmill) and [walking pad benefits](/blog/walking-pad-benefits).
