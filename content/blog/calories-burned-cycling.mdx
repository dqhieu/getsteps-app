# Calories Burned Cycling: Charts by Speed, Time, and Weight

**Cycling burns roughly 400–600 calories per hour** at a moderate pace (12–14 mph) for a 155–180 lb person. Pedal harder at a vigorous pace and you can push past 700 calories an hour; cruise leisurely under 10 mph and you'll land closer to 250–300. Your exact calories burned cycling depends on three things: your body weight, your speed, and the resistance you're working against.

This guide breaks down exactly how many calories cycling burns — outdoor and stationary — with reference charts by weight, intensity, and time. Whether you're spinning indoors, commuting, or chasing a weight-loss goal, you'll know your numbers.

## Quick Answer: Calories Burned Cycling

Here's the short version for an average 155 lb (70 kg) adult:

- **Leisurely cycling (under 10 mph):** ~250 calories/hour
- **Moderate cycling (12–14 mph):** ~480 calories/hour
- **Vigorous cycling (14–16 mph):** ~600 calories/hour
- **Stationary bike, moderate effort:** ~420 calories/hour
- **Stationary bike, vigorous effort:** ~540 calories/hour

A heavier rider burns more at every intensity because moving more mass costs more energy. A 215 lb rider burns roughly 40% more than a 125 lb rider at the same speed and duration.

## How Cycling Calorie Burn Is Calculated

Every reliable calorie estimate uses **MET values** (Metabolic Equivalent of Task) — the standard sports scientists use to compare the energy cost of activities. The formula is:

```
Calories per minute = MET × 3.5 × body weight (kg) ÷ 200
```

To get calories per hour, multiply by 60. One MET equals your resting metabolic rate, so an 8-MET activity burns eight times the energy you'd use sitting still.

Here are the MET values for the most common cycling scenarios:

| Cycling Type | Speed / Effort | MET Value |
|--------------|----------------|-----------|
| Leisurely | Under 10 mph | 4.0 |
| Light commuting | 10–12 mph | 6.0 |
| Moderate | 12–14 mph | 8.0 |
| Vigorous | 14–16 mph | 10.0 |
| Racing / fast | 16–19 mph | 12.0 |
| Stationary bike, moderate | ~100 watts | 6.8 |
| Stationary bike, vigorous | ~150+ watts | 8.8 |

These match the values in the Compendium of Physical Activities, the reference database used worldwide. Notice that moderate outdoor cycling (8 METs) slightly outpaces a moderate stationary session (6.8 METs) — wind resistance and rolling terrain add work that a fixed flywheel doesn't always replicate.

## Calories Burned Cycling in 30 Minutes

If you only have half an hour, cycling still delivers a serious burn. For the average 155 lb rider:

- **Leisurely 30 min:** ~148 calories
- **Moderate 30 min:** ~295 calories
- **Vigorous 30 min:** ~370 calories

A 30-minute moderate ride burns roughly the same as a 45-minute brisk walk — one of the reasons cycling is a favorite for time-crunched workouts. Bump the resistance or chase a few hills and you'll edge a 155 lb rider toward 400 calories in that same half hour. For heavier riders the numbers climb fast: a 215 lb rider can burn over 500 calories in a vigorous 30-minute session.

This makes a daily 30-minute ride a realistic way to create a meaningful calorie deficit without carving out a big block of time.

## Calories Burned Stationary Biking (Indoor Cycling)

Stationary biking — whether on an upright bike, a recumbent, or in a spin class — burns slightly fewer calories than outdoor riding at a matched "feel," because there's no wind, no coasting downhill, and no terrain. But you control the resistance precisely, which makes indoor cycling extremely consistent and easy to track.

Here's the calories burned stationary biking chart, using a moderate MET of 6.8 and a vigorous MET of 8.8:

A **30-minute moderate stationary ride** burns about 251 calories for a 155 lb person, while a hard spin class hour can torch 650+ calories. If your bike has a power meter, you can dial intensity even more precisely: about 100 watts maps to ~6.8 METs, while sustained 150+ watts pushes you toward 8.8 METs and beyond.

## Cycling vs Walking for Calorie Burn

Since cycling and walking are the two most accessible cardio options, it's worth seeing how they stack up. Here's a 60-minute, 155 lb comparison:

| Activity | Intensity | MET | Calories (60 min) |
|----------|-----------|-----|-------------------|
| Walking | 3.5 mph (moderate) | 4.3 | 317 cal |
| Walking | 4.0 mph (brisk) | 5.0 | 369 cal |
| Cycling | Leisurely (under 10 mph) | 4.0 | 295 cal |
| Cycling | Moderate (12–14 mph) | 8.0 | 590 cal |
| Cycling | Vigorous (14–16 mph) | 10.0 | 738 cal |
| Stationary bike | Moderate | 6.8 | 502 cal |

The takeaway: **leisurely cycling burns about the same as moderate walking**, but once you pedal at a moderate-to-vigorous pace, cycling pulls clearly ahead — burning 60–100% more calories per hour than brisk walking. The trade-off is that walking is weight-bearing (better for bone density and incidental daily movement), while cycling is non-impact (easier on joints and great for longer durations).

## What Affects How Many Calories You Burn Cycling

Beyond weight, speed, and resistance, several factors move your calorie burn up or down:

- **Terrain and hills.** Climbing dramatically raises your effort. A hilly route can burn 30–50% more than the same distance on flat ground.
- **Wind and drafting.** Riding into a headwind increases resistance; drafting behind another rider lowers it.
- **Cadence and gearing.** Grinding a heavy gear or spinning a fast cadence both raise intensity compared to easy coasting.
- **Body composition.** More muscle mass slightly raises resting and active calorie burn.
- **Bike type.** A road bike is more efficient than a mountain or fat-tire bike, so off-road riding burns more for the same speed.
- **Fitness level.** Fitter riders are more efficient, but they can also sustain higher intensities — and therefore burn more overall.

## How Long to Cycle to Burn 500 Calories

A common goal is to burn 500 calories in a single ride. Here's roughly how long that takes by intensity for a 155 lb rider:

| Intensity | Approx. Time to Burn 500 cal |
|-----------|------------------------------|
| Leisurely (4.0 MET) | ~1 hr 42 min |
| Moderate (8.0 MET) | ~51 min |
| Vigorous (10.0 MET) | ~41 min |
| Stationary, moderate (6.8 MET) | ~60 min |
| Stationary, vigorous (8.8 MET) | ~46 min |

Heavier riders hit 500 calories faster. A 215 lb rider at a moderate pace needs only about 37 minutes. The simplest path to a 500-calorie ride for most people is a moderate 50–60 minute session — sustainable, repeatable, and easy on the joints.

## Using Cycling for Weight Loss

To lose one pound of fat you need roughly a 3,500-calorie deficit. Three moderate one-hour rides per week (about 590 calories each for a 155 lb rider) burn around 1,770 calories — enough that, paired with modest diet changes, you can lose about half a pound a week from cycling alone.

Consistency beats intensity here. A daily 30-minute ride you actually keep up with will outperform an occasional brutal session you dread. Track your rides alongside your steps so you can see the full picture of your weekly movement.

## FAQ

### How many calories does cycling burn?

Cycling burns roughly **400–600 calories per hour** at a moderate pace (12–14 mph) for a 155–180 lb adult. Leisurely riding under 10 mph burns about 250–350 calories per hour, while vigorous riding at 14–16 mph can exceed 700. Your weight, speed, and resistance are the biggest variables.

### How many calories does cycling burn in 30 minutes?

A 155 lb person burns about **148 calories leisurely, 295 calories at a moderate pace, and 370 calories at a vigorous pace** in 30 minutes. Heavier riders burn more — a 215 lb rider can top 500 calories in a vigorous 30-minute ride.

### Does stationary biking burn as many calories as outdoor cycling?

Not quite. At a matched effort, stationary biking burns slightly fewer calories because there's no wind resistance, no coasting, and no terrain. A moderate stationary session uses about 6.8 METs versus 8.0 METs for moderate outdoor riding. That said, you can match or exceed outdoor burn indoors by raising the resistance, so the gap closes if you push harder.

### Is cycling or walking better for weight loss?

Both work, but they suit different needs. Moderate-to-vigorous cycling burns 60–100% more calories per hour than brisk walking, making it more time-efficient for a calorie deficit. Walking is weight-bearing and easier to fit into everyday life. For many people, combining both — cycling for dedicated workouts and walking for daily movement — produces the best, most sustainable results.

### How long should I cycle to burn 500 calories?

A 155 lb rider burns 500 calories in about **51 minutes at a moderate pace** or **41 minutes at a vigorous pace**. On a stationary bike, plan on roughly 60 minutes at moderate effort or 46 minutes at vigorous effort. Heavier riders reach 500 calories faster.

### Why do heavier people burn more calories cycling?

Moving more body mass requires more energy, so a heavier rider expends more calories at the same speed and duration. A 25% increase in body weight produces roughly a 25% increase in calories burned for the same ride. This is why every chart above scales up with weight.
