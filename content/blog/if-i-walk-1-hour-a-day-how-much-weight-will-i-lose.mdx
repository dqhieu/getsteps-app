# If I Walk 1 Hour a Day, How Much Weight Will I Lose? (2026)

**If you walk 1 hour a day and change nothing else, expect to lose roughly 0.5 to 1 pound per week at first — about 2 to 3 pounds in the first month and 4 to 8 pounds by month three. Heavier bodies and brisker paces sit at the top of that range. The loss slows after roughly 8 to 12 weeks, and that slowdown is normal, not failure.**

That is the honest answer to *if I walk 1 hour a day how much weight will I lose*. Below is the arithmetic behind it, a month-by-month projection for three body weights, and a blunt explanation of why plenty of people walk an hour a day for two months and see the scale refuse to move.

## Step 1: How Many Calories a 1-Hour Walk Actually Burns

Everything downstream depends on this number, so it's worth getting right. Calorie burn is calculated from MET values published in the Compendium of Physical Activities:

**Calories = MET × body weight (kg) × hours**

Walking at 3.0 mph is 3.5 METs, 3.5 mph is 4.3 METs, and 4.0 mph is 5.0 METs.

| Body Weight | 3.0 mph (moderate) | 3.5 mph (brisk) | 4.0 mph (fast) | Net burn at 3.5 mph |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 140 lb (64 kg) | 222 cal | 273 cal | 318 cal | **210 cal** |
| 160 lb (73 kg) | 254 cal | 312 cal | 363 cal | **240 cal** |
| 180 lb (82 kg) | 286 cal | 351 cal | 408 cal | **269 cal** |
| 200 lb (91 kg) | 317 cal | 390 cal | 454 cal | **299 cal** |
| 220 lb (100 kg) | 349 cal | 429 cal | 499 cal | **329 cal** |
| 250 lb (113 kg) | 397 cal | 488 cal | 567 cal | **374 cal** |

That last column is the one almost every article skips, and it's the reason predictions run high. The first three columns are **gross** burn — the total energy your body used during that hour, including the 70 to 100 calories you would have burned lying on the sofa anyway. The **net** figure subtracts your resting metabolism, and net is what actually adds to your deficit. Using gross numbers inflates your projected loss by 20 to 25%.

## Step 2: Turn That Into a Weekly Deficit

Walk seven days a week at a brisk 3.5 mph and the net numbers above become a weekly deficit:

- **160 lb:** 240 × 7 = **1,680 calories per week**
- **200 lb:** 299 × 7 = **2,093 calories per week**
- **250 lb:** 374 × 7 = **2,618 calories per week**

One pound of body fat stores roughly 3,500 calories. Divide through and you get **0.48, 0.60, and 0.75 pounds per week** respectively. That is the ceiling from walking alone, assuming your diet holds perfectly steady.

Notice what this rules out. If you are asking *if I walk 1 hour a day how much weight will I lose*, and you're hoping for 2 pounds a week, an hour of walking cannot produce that on its own at any realistic body weight. The [calorie deficit calculator](/tools/calorie-deficit-calculator) shows what you'd need on the food side to close that gap.

## If I Walk 1 Hour a Day, How Much Weight Will I Lose in 1, 3, and 6 Months?

Here's where the 3,500-calorie rule starts to break. It assumes your deficit stays constant forever, and it doesn't — you get lighter, so each walk burns less, and your body defends its weight. Researchers at the NIH, led by Kevin Hall, have shown the linear rule overestimates real long-term loss by roughly a factor of two.

Both columns below assume a brisk 1-hour walk every day and no deliberate diet change:

| Body Weight | | 1 month | 3 months | 6 months |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **160 lb** | Linear (3,500 rule) | 2.1 lb | 6.2 lb | 12.5 lb |
| | Realistic | **1.5–2 lb** | **4–5 lb** | **6–8 lb** |
| **200 lb** | Linear (3,500 rule) | 2.6 lb | 7.8 lb | 15.5 lb |
| | Realistic | **2–2.5 lb** | **5–6 lb** | **8–10 lb** |
| **250 lb** | Linear (3,500 rule) | 3.2 lb | 9.7 lb | 19.4 lb |
| | Realistic | **2.5–3 lb** | **6–8 lb** | **10–12 lb** |

The realistic column is the one to plan around. It reflects Hall's dynamic model: a sustained daily deficit produces about half its "predicted" loss within the first year, with the curve flattening as you go.

So the full answer to *if I walk 1 hour a day how much weight will I lose* over six months is **6 to 12 pounds from the walking alone**, depending on where you start. Model your own curve with the [Weight Loss Walking Calculator](/tools/weight-loss-walking-calculator).

## Why Some People Walk 1 Hour a Day and Lose Nothing

This is the common experience, and it has four ordinary explanations. None of them mean walking doesn't work.

**1. You ate the deficit back.** A 200-lb person nets about 299 calories from an hour of brisk walking. A medium latte and a granola bar is 400. Appetite genuinely rises with activity, and the compensation is usually unconscious rather than a decision you'd remember making. Studies of exercise-only interventions consistently find people recover 25 to 50% of the calories they burned through extra eating.

**2. Water and glycogen are masking fat loss.** New exercise causes muscles to store more glycogen, and each gram of glycogen holds roughly 3 grams of water. In weeks one and two this can add 2 to 4 pounds to the scale while you are simultaneously losing fat. Your waistband will tell you the truth before the scale does.

**3. You hit the month-two plateau.** As you lose weight, each walk burns fewer calories — a 190-lb version of you burns about 5% less per hour than the 200-lb version. Resting metabolism drifts down too. Deficit shrinks, loss slows. This typically becomes obvious around weeks 8 to 12.

**4. The hour replaced other movement.** People who add a formal walk often sit more the rest of the day. Total daily step count barely changes even though a 60-minute walk got added. Tracking all-day steps rather than just the walk is the only way to catch this.

The fix for a plateau is not walking longer. It's re-checking your maintenance calories at your new weight with a [TDEE calculator](/tools/tdee-calculator), then either adding a small food reduction or picking up the pace. See [does walking reduce belly fat](/blog/does-walking-reduce-belly-fat) for what shifts underneath a stalled scale.

## How to Get to 1–2 lb a Week Instead

Walking an hour is roughly half of a 1-lb-per-week plan. The other half comes from food:

| Setup | Weekly deficit (200 lb) | Weekly loss |
|---|---|---|
| 1-hour walk only | ~2,100 cal | ~0.5 lb |
| 1-hour walk + 250 cal/day cut | ~3,850 cal | ~1.1 lb |
| 1-hour walk + 500 cal/day cut | ~5,600 cal | ~1.6 lb |

The CDC considers 1 to 2 pounds per week the safe, sustainable rate, and that middle row is the sweet spot most people can hold for months. A 250-calorie daily cut is one snack or one drink — small enough that it doesn't feel like dieting, which is exactly why it survives past week three.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### If I walk 1 hour a day, how much weight will I lose in a month?

About **1.5 to 3 pounds** from walking alone, depending on body weight and pace. A 160-lb person walking briskly nets roughly 1,680 calories a week, or a little under 2 pounds a month. A 250-lb person nets closer to 3 pounds. Add a 250-calorie daily food reduction and the monthly figure roughly doubles.

### Is 1 hour of walking a day enough to lose weight?

Yes, but slowly. It reliably produces 0.5 to 0.75 pounds per week at first without any diet change, and it comfortably exceeds the 150 weekly minutes of moderate activity recommended by the CDC and American Heart Association. If you want 1 to 2 pounds a week, walking supplies about half the deficit and food has to supply the rest.

### Why am I not losing weight walking 1 hour a day?

The four usual causes are eating back the deficit (appetite rises with activity), water retention from new glycogen storage in the first two weeks, the natural plateau around month two as your lighter body burns less, and sitting more the rest of the day. Track total daily steps and food for one honest week and the culprit almost always shows up.

### Will I lose belly fat walking an hour a day?

You'll lose fat from everywhere, including the abdomen — you just can't direct it. Visceral belly fat is metabolically active and tends to respond early to regular aerobic activity, which is why waist measurements often drop before the scale does. Measure your waist monthly rather than relying only on weight.

### Does walking faster make a big difference?

A meaningful one. At 200 lb, going from 3.0 mph to 4.0 mph raises the hour's burn from 317 to 454 calories — a 43% increase for the same time invested. Over six months that difference alone is worth several pounds. If you can only spare an hour, spend it walking briskly.

## The Bottom Line

An hour of daily walking is a real but modest weight-loss tool: **roughly 0.5 to 1 pound a week early on, 4 to 8 pounds by three months, and 6 to 12 pounds by six months** if your eating stays constant. It is not a fast method, and anyone promising 20 pounds in three months from walking alone is selling something.

What it is, is durable. The deficit is small enough that it doesn't trigger the hunger rebound that sinks aggressive diets, and the habit survives holidays and bad weeks. Pair it with a 250-calorie daily food reduction and you land squarely in the CDC's 1-to-2-pounds-a-week range without ever feeling like you're on a diet.
