# Walk Run Method: 8-Week Plan and Ratios by Goal

**The walk run method alternates short running segments with walk breaks you schedule from the very first minute.** You do not wait until you are exhausted to walk. A beginner might run 30 seconds and walk 90 seconds. A marathoner might run 4 minutes and walk 1. The walk breaks keep effort steady, give the legs a short recovery, and make it possible to cover more distance than you could by running continuously.

Jeff Galloway made the walk run method famous under the name **run-walk-run**, which is why many runners call it "Jeffing." This guide covers how the method works, an 8-week walk-to-run plan, walk run intervals by race goal, how to time the switches, and how the sessions add to your daily step count.

## What Is the Walk Run Method?

The walk run method is a structured interval approach. You pick a ratio, such as 1 minute running to 1 minute walking, and repeat it for the whole session. The walks are planned and regular, not a sign that you have failed.

Three rules make it work:

1. **Walk early.** The first walk break comes in the first few minutes, while you still feel fresh.
2. **Keep the ratio steady.** Repeat the same run and walk segments for the session, even if you feel strong halfway through.
3. **Run easy.** The running segments are conversational. The walk breaks are not a license to sprint the runs.

Galloway, a 1972 US Olympian in the 10,000 meters, started using scheduled walk breaks in 1974 with a beginner running class. He wanted new runners to finish sessions without getting hurt or burned out. Over the following decades he applied the same idea to marathon training, and his run-walk-run method became one of the most widely used beginner approaches in recreational running. Galloway died in February 2026 at age 80, according to his family, as reported by [Atlanta News First](https://www.atlantanewsfirst.com/2026/02/26/jeff-galloway-first-peachtree-road-race-winner-olympian-dies-80/).

## Why the Walk Run Method Works

Continuous running loads the same muscles, tendons, and joints with every stride. A walk break shifts the work to a gentler pattern for a minute, then you start running again. The idea is to spread fatigue out over the whole session instead of letting it pile up.

The evidence is limited but useful. A 2016 randomized study in the *Journal of Science and Medicine in Sport* by Hottenrott and colleagues had 42 non-elite runners finish a marathon either running only or with a run/walk strategy. Finish times were similar between groups. The run/walk group reported **less muscle pain and fatigue** after the race. The walk breaks did not reduce markers of cardiac stress, so the benefit was muscular comfort, not an easier load on the heart.

That matches how most people use the walk run method:

- **Beginners** use it to build running time without the "I can't breathe after two minutes" wall.
- **Runners returning from a layoff or injury** use it to reintroduce impact gradually, once a clinician has cleared them to run.
- **Half and full marathoners** use it to hold an even effort and finish the last miles in better shape.

## Walk Run Method vs Straight Running

| | Walk run method | Continuous running |
|---|---|---|
| Effort | Steady, with built-in recovery | Builds steadily through the run |
| Best for | Beginners, returners, long distances | Runners who already handle the distance |
| How fatigue builds | Spread across the session | Accumulates without breaks |
| Typical pace | A bit slower per mile in short races | Faster in short races for trained runners |
| Learning curve | Need a timer and a plan | Just go |

Straight running is not "better." It is a different tool. If you can already run a 5K comfortably, you may not need walk breaks for that distance. If you are building toward a half or full marathon, many runners keep the walk breaks even after they could run straight through.

For the calorie side of the comparison, see [walking vs running for weight loss](/blog/walking-vs-running-for-weight-loss).

## 8-Week Walk to Run Plan

This walk-to-run plan uses three sessions a week, on non-consecutive days. Each session starts with a **5-minute brisk walk** and ends with a **3-minute easy walk**. Sessions land between 24 and 29 minutes.

| Week | Run / walk interval | Repeats | Running time | Session total |
|------|---------------------|---------|--------------|---------------|
| 1 | Run 30 sec / walk 90 sec | 8 | 4 min | 24 min |
| 2 | Run 1 min / walk 2 min | 6 | 6 min | 26 min |
| 3 | Run 1 min / walk 1 min | 10 | 10 min | 28 min |
| 4 | Run 2 min / walk 1 min | 6 | 12 min | 26 min |
| 5 | Run 3 min / walk 1 min | 5 | 15 min | 28 min |
| 6 | Run 4 min / walk 1 min | 4 | 16 min | 28 min |
| 7 | Run 5 min / walk 1 min | 3 | 15 min | 26 min |
| 8 | Run 6 min / walk 1 min | 3 | 18 min | 29 min |

How to use the plan:

- **Repeat any week that felt hard.** Moving on is optional. Finishing all three sessions comfortably is the signal to progress.
- **Rest days matter.** Walk on the off days if you like, but leave a day between run sessions.
- **Do not stretch the run segments mid-session.** If week 4 feels easy, finish it as written and start week 5 next time.

By week 8 you are running 18 minutes per session. Add a repeat or two over the following weeks and a 5K with the walk run method becomes a realistic next goal. Distance details are in [how many miles is a 5K](/blog/how-many-miles-is-a-5k).

If you have not been walking regularly yet, start with a walking base first. The [walking workout plan for beginners](/blog/walking-workout-plan-beginners) builds that in four weeks.

## Walk Run Intervals by Goal

Once the 8-week plan is done, choose a ratio for the distance you are training for. These are common starting ranges, not fixed rules. Galloway's own charts tie the ratio to your pace: the slower your easy pace, the more often you walk.

| Goal | Common run / walk ratio | Notes |
|------|-------------------------|-------|
| First weeks of running | 30 sec / 30 sec to 1 min / 1 min | Focus on finishing every session |
| 5K | 2 min / 1 min to 4 min / 1 min | Some runners drop the breaks for the last mile |
| 10K | 3 min / 1 min to 5 min / 1 min | Same ratio in training and on race day |
| Half marathon | 4 min / 1 min to 6 min / 1 min | Short 30-second breaks work for some runners |
| Marathon | 3 min / 1 min to 5 min / 1 min | Start conservative; walk breaks help most in the final miles |

A **marathon** is 26.2 miles, so even a small saving in fatigue per mile adds up. See [how many miles is a marathon](/blog/how-many-miles-is-a-marathon) and [how many steps in a half marathon](/blog/how-many-steps-in-a-half-marathon) for the scale of each race.

To turn a ratio into a finish time, plug your average pace into the [Race Time Predictor](/tools/race-time-predictor) or the [Running Pace Calculator](/tools/running-pace-calculator). Use your overall pace, walks included, not your running-segment pace.

## How Fast to Walk and Run

Pace is where the walk run method succeeds or falls apart.

**Walk briskly.** The walk is active recovery, not a stroll. Swing the arms, keep a quick turnover, and aim for the effort described in [what is brisk walking](/blog/what-is-brisk-walking). Good posture helps here too: [how to walk properly](/blog/how-to-walk-properly).

**Run easy.** You should be able to talk in full sentences during the running segments. If you are gasping, slow down. For many beginners, that easy running pace is not much faster than a fast walk, and that is fine. Typical easy paces are in [average jogging speed](/blog/average-jogging-speed).

**Check it with numbers if you like.** The [Training Pace Zones](/tools/training-pace-zones) tool gives easy-pace ranges from a recent race time. If you train by heart rate, the easy effort sits near zone 2, explained in [zone 2 walking](/blog/zone-2-walking) and the [Heart Rate Zones Calculator](/tools/heart-rate-zones-calculator).

The most common mistake is running the segments too hard because a walk is coming. That turns the session into hard intervals, a different workout. If you want structured fast-and-slow walking instead, the [Japanese interval walking method](/blog/japanese-interval-walking-method) is a walking-only option.

## How to Time Your Intervals

You need something that tells you when to switch. Checking a clock every 30 seconds gets old fast.

- **GPS running watches.** Many have a run/walk or interval setting that buzzes at each switch.
- **Apple Watch.** The Workout app supports custom workouts with alternating work and recovery intervals for outdoor runs.
- **Interval timer apps.** Any phone timer that repeats two intervals with a sound or vibration will do the job.
- **Landmarks.** On a familiar route, run between lampposts or blocks. Less precise, but no gear needed.

Set the timer before you leave the door.

## How the Walk Run Method Fits Your Step Goal

A walk run session adds a lot of steps in a short time. This is arithmetic, not a measurement: brisk walking is often around 100 to 120 steps per minute, and easy running for recreational runners commonly lands around 150 to 170.

Take week 5 of the plan (8 minutes of warm-up and cool-down walking, 5 minutes of walk breaks, 15 minutes of running):

- **Walking:** 13 minutes × 110 steps = about 1,430 steps
- **Running:** 15 minutes × 160 steps = about 2,400 steps
- **Total:** roughly **3,800 steps** in 28 minutes

Three of those sessions a week add more than 11,000 steps. A 5K covered with the walk run method sits between walking and running step counts, because running strides are longer. The details are in [how many steps in a 5K](/blog/how-many-steps-in-a-5k) and [how many steps in a mile running](/blog/how-many-steps-in-a-mile-running).

The [Steps app](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/steps-workout-pedometer/id6746096378) counts from iPhone and Apple Watch, so the running segments, walk breaks, and the rest of your day land in one daily total.

## Tips for Sticking With the Walk Run Method

- **Take the first walk break early,** even if you feel great. That is the whole point.
- **Keep the same ratio for a full week** before changing it.
- **Watch for warning signs.** Pain along the inner shin or tight, sore calves often means too much too soon. See [shin splints from walking](/blog/shin-splints-from-walking) and [calf pain when walking](/blog/calf-pain-when-walking). Repeat a week or take an extra rest day.
- **Do not let weather break the streak.** A light rain run is fine with the right layers: [walking in the rain](/blog/walking-in-the-rain).
- **Use the same intervals on race day** that you used in training.

## Common Questions

### What is the Jeff Galloway run walk run method?

It is a training and racing approach where you alternate running with short, scheduled walk breaks from the start of every session. Jeff Galloway introduced it to a beginner class in 1974. Common ratios range from 30 seconds running and 30 seconds walking up to several minutes of running with a 1-minute walk.

### What is a good walk run interval for beginners?

Start with **30 seconds of running and 90 seconds of walking**, or 1 minute of each if that feels easy. Repeat for about 15 to 20 minutes after a 5-minute brisk walk warm-up. Lengthen the running segment by a minute every week or two.

### Is the walk run method slower than running?

Not always. In a 2016 randomized marathon study of 42 non-elite runners, finish times were similar between a run/walk group and a running-only group, and the run/walk group reported less muscle pain and fatigue. In short races, trained runners are usually a bit faster running continuously.

### How long does it take to go from walking to running?

Many beginners can run several minutes at a time after about 8 weeks of three walk run sessions a week. Some take longer. Repeating a week is normal and often the smarter choice.

### Can you use the walk run method for a marathon?

Yes. Many marathoners use ratios between 3 minutes running / 1 minute walking and 5 minutes running / 1 minute walking. Practice the same ratio on your long runs so race day feels familiar.

### Does walk run count toward my daily steps?

Yes. Both the running and walking segments count. A 28-minute walk run session is roughly 3,800 steps by arithmetic, depending on your cadence and how much of the session is running.

## Start Your First Walk Run Session

The walk run method is the simplest way to go from walking to running without overdoing it. Walk briskly, run easy, take your walk breaks early, and repeat the same ratio for a week before you change it. Eight weeks of three sessions gets many beginners to 18 minutes of running per session, a solid base for a first 5K.

**This article is general information, not medical advice.** Talk to a clinician before starting if you have heart, joint, or other health conditions, or if you are returning from an injury.
