# How Many Miles Do I Need to Walk to Lose 5 Pounds a Week?

**To lose 5 pounds a week you need a 17,500-calorie weekly deficit — about 2,500 calories a day. From walking alone that works out to roughly 27 to 33 miles every single day at typical adult body weights, or about 190 to 230 miles a week. That is 9 to 11 hours of walking daily, and no, it is not something almost anyone can or should do.**

That is the real answer to "how many miles do I need to walk to lose 5 pounds a week," and most pages either dodge it or quietly swap in a smaller goal. Below is the arithmetic that produces the number, why the true figure is even higher than it looks, and what walking genuinely delivers when you point it at a target it can hit.

## How Many Miles to Walk to Lose 5 Pounds a Week: The Arithmetic

One pound of body fat stores roughly **3,500 calories**. Five pounds is therefore about **17,500 calories** — a deficit you'd have to create in seven days, or **2,500 calories every day**.

For most adults, 2,500 calories a day is close to their *entire* daily energy expenditure — a moderately active 185 lb adult burns roughly 2,400–2,700 calories total. Losing 5 pounds a week by walking means adding a second full day's worth of burn on top of the day you're already living.

Here's that deficit next to the more common targets, with the mileage each requires at three body weights. Walking burns roughly **0.5 calories per pound of body weight per mile**, so a 150 lb walker burns about 75 calories a mile and a 250 lb walker about 125.

| Weekly loss | Weekly deficit | Daily deficit | Miles/day at 150 lb | Miles/day at 185 lb | Miles/day at 250 lb |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 lb | 3,500 cal | 500 cal | 6.7 mi | 5.4 mi | 4.0 mi |
| 2 lb | 7,000 cal | 1,000 cal | 13.3 mi | 10.8 mi | 8.0 mi |
| 3 lb | 10,500 cal | 1,500 cal | 20.0 mi | 16.2 mi | 12.0 mi |
| **5 lb** | **17,500 cal** | **2,500 cal** | **33.3 mi** | **27.0 mi** | **20.0 mi** |

At a normal 3 mph walking pace, 33 miles takes **11 hours**. Every day. Seven days a week. Add sleep and you have about five waking hours left for work, food, family, and everything else.

## Why the Miles You Need to Walk Are Even Higher

The 0.5-calories-per-pound-per-mile figure is **gross** calorie burn: everything your body spends while walking, including the calories you'd have burned anyway just sitting there breathing. Only the **net** burn above resting creates a deficit, and net burn is closer to **0.3 calories per pound per mile**.

Run the 5-pound target on net burn and it gets uglier:

- **150 lb:** ~45 net cal/mile → **55 miles a day**
- **185 lb:** ~56 net cal/mile → **45 miles a day**
- **250 lb:** ~75 net cal/mile → **33 miles a day**

So the honest range for "how many miles do I need to walk to lose 5 pounds a week" is **27 miles a day at the flattering end and 55 at the accurate end**. Ultramarathoners cover that kind of distance. They do not do it daily, and they eat enormously to stay upright.

Two more things work against the target even if you could log the miles. Walking a lot makes you hungrier, and appetite compensation reliably claws back part of what you burned. And as you get lighter, every mile burns less — the plan gets harder exactly as it succeeds.

## Can You Lose 5 Pounds in a Week At All?

Sometimes, briefly, and mostly not as fat.

**People at high starting body weights often do drop 3–5 pounds in the first week or two** of a new eating and walking routine. That is real and it shows on the scale. It is also mostly **water and glycogen**, not fat. Every gram of stored glycogen holds roughly 3 grams of water with it, and cutting carbohydrates or calories drains that store fast. Cut sodium at the same time and the drop is bigger still.

That loss is a one-time event. It does not repeat in week three, and when people expect it to, they conclude the plan stopped working and quit. It didn't stop working — the water was already gone.

For sustained loss, the **CDC** and **Mayo Clinic** both recommend **1 to 2 pounds a week**, created by a 500–750 calorie daily deficit. Mayo Clinic is explicit that faster long-term loss costs you muscle along with fat and is harder to keep off. Anything sustained beyond roughly 1% of body weight per week belongs under medical supervision, not a walking plan.

## What Walking Can Actually Deliver: The Realistic Plan

Here is the useful version of the question. Walking will not produce 5 pounds a week, but combined with a moderate diet cut it produces 1–2 pounds a week comfortably — which is 5 pounds in **three to four weeks** rather than one.

These figures assume a 185 lb walker at roughly 93 calories per mile. Heavier walkers burn more per mile; lighter walkers burn less.

| Miles walked/day | Walking deficit | Diet deficit | Total daily deficit | Weekly loss |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 mi (~4,500 steps) | ~185 cal | 0 | ~185 cal | ~0.4 lb |
| 3 mi (~6,700 steps) | ~280 cal | 250 cal | ~530 cal | ~1.1 lb |
| 4 mi (~9,000 steps) | ~370 cal | 300 cal | ~670 cal | ~1.3 lb |
| 5 mi (~11,000 steps) | ~465 cal | 500 cal | ~965 cal | ~1.9 lb |
| 6 mi (~13,500 steps) | ~560 cal | 500 cal | ~1,060 cal | ~2.1 lb |

The pattern is clear: **diet carries roughly half the deficit in every workable plan.** Walking 6 miles a day with no dietary change nets about 1.1 lb a week and takes over two hours. Walking 4 miles and cutting 300 calories nets slightly more in an hour and change — the trade most people should make.

Note also the ceiling. Even 6 miles a day plus a solid 500-calorie cut lands at ~2.1 lb/week, the top of the range the CDC calls sustainable. No row in this table reaches 5. That isn't pessimism; it's what the numbers do.

## Getting to 5 Pounds Faster Without Breaking Anything

If 5 pounds is the goal and speed matters, these are the levers that genuinely move the timeline:

**Walk briskly, not casually.** Going from 2.5 mph to 3.5 mph raises calorie burn per mile by roughly 15–20% and cuts the time cost substantially.

**Add incline.** A 5% grade increases burn by 30–40% at the same speed. A treadmill at 3 mph and 5% incline does meaningfully more work than 3 mph flat.

**Split the walks.** Three 20-minute walks are easier to actually complete than one 60-minute block, and post-meal walks blunt blood-sugar spikes as a bonus.

**Cut liquid calories first.** One sweetened drink a day is often 200–350 calories with no satiety return — the cheapest deficit available.

**Keep protein high and lift something.** Preserving muscle during a deficit keeps your daily burn from sagging as the weight comes off.

Do all five and 5 pounds arrives in about three weeks instead of one. It also stays gone, which the one-week version never manages.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How many miles do I need to walk to lose 5 pounds a week?

About **27 to 33 miles a day** at typical adult body weights using standard calorie-per-mile estimates, and closer to **45–55 miles a day** once you account for net rather than gross calorie burn. That's 190–380 miles a week and 9 to 15 hours of walking daily. Walking alone cannot realistically produce 5 pounds of weekly fat loss for almost anyone.

### Is losing 5 pounds a week safe?

Not as a sustained target. The CDC and Mayo Clinic both recommend 1–2 pounds per week. Losing faster over an extended period typically costs muscle mass, is hard to maintain, and is associated with rebound weight gain. Rapid loss beyond that range belongs under medical supervision.

### But I lost 5 pounds in my first week — how?

Mostly water and glycogen, especially if you cut carbohydrates or sodium at the same time. Glycogen stores hold about three times their weight in water, and they drain quickly at the start of a new routine. People at higher starting weights see the biggest first-week drop. It's real weight, but it doesn't repeat, and it isn't 17,500 calories of fat.

### How long does it realistically take to lose 5 pounds by walking?

At 1–2 pounds a week, **three to five weeks** with walking plus a moderate calorie deficit. Walking 4–5 miles a day and cutting 300–500 calories from your diet is the standard route. Walking with no dietary change stretches it to roughly six to twelve weeks. See our [walking to lose weight chart](/blog/walking-to-lose-weight-chart) and [how many steps to lose a pound](/blog/how-many-steps-to-lose-a-pound) for the step-count version of the same math.

### How many miles a day should I actually aim for?

For most people, **4 to 6 miles a day** — roughly 9,000 to 13,500 steps — paired with a 300–500 calorie dietary trim. That reliably produces 1.3 to 2.1 pounds a week, sits inside the safe range, and takes about an hour to ninety minutes. Our [walking schedule for weight loss](/blog/walking-schedule-for-weight-loss) lays out an eight-week ramp to that volume.

## The Bottom Line

The number you came for is **27–33 miles a day**, and once you account for net calorie burn, closer to 45–55. The reason nobody hits it is that it requires more hours than a day contains.

Walking isn't the wrong tool — it's pointed at the wrong number. Four to six miles a day plus a moderate calorie cut puts 5 pounds behind you in three to four weeks, keeps your muscle, and doesn't require quitting your job. Set the target where the math works and walking will hold up its end.
