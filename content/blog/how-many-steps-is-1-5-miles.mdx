# How Many Steps Is 1.5 Miles? Chart by Height

**How many steps is 1.5 miles? About 3,450 steps for the average adult walking at a normal pace, or roughly 2,400 steps running. Most walkers land between 3,000 and 3,850 steps depending on height — shorter walkers take closer to 3,800, taller walkers closer to 3,100. Walking 1.5 miles takes about 30 minutes.**

1.5 miles is a distance you meet more often than you'd think. It's the standard timed-run event for the Navy and Air Force fitness tests, the Cooper-standard run used by most police and fire academies, a common treadmill benchmark, and a fixture of school PE testing. Here's exactly what it costs you in steps.

## The Short Answer

| Pace | Speed | Steps in 1.5 Miles | Time |
|------|-------|-------------------|------|
| Slow stroll | 2.0 mph | 3,750–3,900 | 45 min |
| Casual walk | 2.5 mph | 3,540–3,690 | 36 min |
| Normal walk | 3.0 mph | 3,300–3,450 | 30 min |
| Brisk walk | 3.5 mph | 3,150–3,300 | 25 min 43 sec |
| Power walk | 4.0 mph | 3,000–3,150 | 22 min 30 sec |
| Easy jog | 5.0 mph | 2,400–2,700 | 18 min |
| Run | 6.0 mph | 2,250–2,500 | 15 min |

Use **3,450 steps** if you want one number to plan around. That's the midpoint for an average-height adult walking at an ordinary pace.

Notice that the step count falls as you speed up. That isn't a rounding artifact — your stride physically lengthens as you move faster, so a fast 1.5 miles costs you several hundred fewer steps than a slow one.

## Steps in 1.5 Miles by Height (Walking and Running)

Height is the biggest single variable. Your walking step length runs about 41–43% of your height; your running step length is far longer, typically around 60% of height at a moderate running pace. Longer step, fewer steps for the same distance.

| Height | Walking Step | Steps Walking | Running Step | Steps Running |
|--------|-------------|---------------|--------------|---------------|
| 5'0" (152 cm) | 24.8" | **3,830** | 36.0" | **2,640** |
| 5'2" (157 cm) | 25.6" | **3,710** | 37.2" | **2,555** |
| 5'4" (163 cm) | 26.4" | **3,600** | 38.4" | **2,475** |
| 5'6" (168 cm) | 27.3" | **3,480** | 39.6" | **2,400** |
| 5'8" (173 cm) | 28.1" | **3,380** | 40.8" | **2,330** |
| 5'10" (178 cm) | 28.9" | **3,290** | 42.0" | **2,260** |
| 6'0" (183 cm) | 29.7" | **3,200** | 43.2" | **2,200** |
| 6'2" (188 cm) | 30.6" | **3,105** | 44.4" | **2,140** |
| 6'4" (193 cm) | 31.4" | **3,025** | 45.6" | **2,085** |

Two things stand out. First, the spread from 5'0" to 6'4" is about 800 steps — a 27% swing. Any generic "1.5 miles = 3,000 steps" figure is badly wrong for anyone under about 5'8".

Second, **running 1.5 miles costs roughly 30% fewer steps than walking it**, because each running step covers about half again as much ground. A 5'6" adult takes about 3,480 steps walking the distance and roughly 2,400 running it. If you use a step goal rather than a distance goal, that difference matters: a 1.5-mile run does far less for your step total than a 1.5-mile walk.

## The Math Behind It

1.5 miles is 7,920 feet, or **95,040 inches**. One division gets you the answer:

**95,040 ÷ your step length in inches = steps in 1.5 miles**

At 5'6", a typical walking step length is 27.3 inches:

95,040 ÷ 27.3 = **3,481 steps**

The same 5'6" adult running with a 39.6-inch step:

95,040 ÷ 39.6 = **2,400 steps**

To measure your own step length instead of estimating it: walk 20 normal steps, measure the total distance in inches, and divide by 20. That single measurement makes every distance-to-step conversion you'll ever do more accurate. It's also the fastest way to check whether your tracker's default stride setting is inflating or deflating your daily mileage.

## How Many Steps Is 1.5 Miles on a Fitness Test?

This is the distance's real claim to fame. 1.5 miles is the timed cardio event on several major fitness assessments:

- **US Navy PRT** — 1.5-mile timed run (with approved alternates including a 500-yard swim and a 2,000-meter row)
- **US Air Force PFA** — 1.5-mile timed run, or the 20-meter HAMR shuttle run as an alternate cardio option
- **US Coast Guard** — 1.5-mile run as the standard aerobic assessment
- **Police and fire academies** — the Cooper Institute 1.5-mile run is the most widely used law-enforcement aerobic standard in the US
- **School PE testing** — a long-running staple of secondary-school fitness benchmarks

One common mix-up worth clearing up: **the US Army does not use 1.5 miles.** The Army Fitness Test uses a **2-mile** run, as does the Space Force; the Marine Corps PFT uses 3 miles. If you're training for the Army, see [how many steps in 2 miles](/blog/how-many-steps-in-2-miles) instead.

### Typical 1.5-mile target times

Standards vary by age, sex, and agency, and official charts get revised — always check the current scoring table for your specific test. These are representative published minimums to calibrate against:

| Test | Group | Approx. passing time | Pace | Steps (approx.) |
|------|-------|---------------------|------|----------------|
| Navy PRT | Male, 20–24 | ~13:30 | 9:00/mi | ~2,400 |
| Navy PRT | Female, 20–24 | ~15:30 | 10:20/mi | ~2,700 |

The practical takeaway for anyone training: a passing 1.5-mile run is roughly **2,300–2,800 steps in 12 to 16 minutes**, which means a cadence in the 170–180 steps-per-minute range. If you're running at 155 spm and struggling with the time cut, raising your cadence toward 175 is usually a faster fix than trying to force a longer stride. ## How Long Does It Take to Walk 1.5 Miles?

At the average adult walking speed of 3 mph, 1.5 miles takes **30 minutes**.

| Pace | Speed | Time for 1.5 Miles |
|------|-------|-------------------|
| Slow | 2.0 mph | 45 minutes |
| Casual | 2.5 mph | 36 minutes |
| Average | 3.0 mph | 30 minutes |
| Brisk | 3.5 mph | 25 min 43 sec |
| Fast | 4.0 mph | 22 min 30 sec |
| Very brisk | 4.5 mph | 20 minutes |
| Jog | 5.0 mph | 18 minutes |

Thirty minutes is a useful coincidence. The CDC recommends 150 minutes of moderate-intensity activity per week, which works out to 30 minutes on five days. A brisk 1.5-mile walk is almost exactly one of those daily sessions — so five 1.5-mile walks a week meets the guideline on its own, at about 17,000 steps of weekly volume.

The track number is the handy one: **six laps of a standard outdoor track is 2,400 meters, just 14 meters short of 1.5 miles.** Most fitness tests simply round to six laps, or use 7.5 laps of a 200 m indoor track. ## Calories Burned Walking 1.5 Miles

Walking burns roughly 0.03 calories per pound of body weight per minute at a moderate pace. Over a 30-minute 1.5-mile walk:

| Body Weight | Calories (Walking, 30 min) | Calories (Running, 15 min) |
|-------------|---------------------------|---------------------------|
| 120 lbs (54 kg) | 108 | ~140 |
| 150 lbs (68 kg) | 135 | ~175 |
| 180 lbs (82 kg) | 162 | ~210 |
| 200 lbs (91 kg) | 180 | ~235 |
| 250 lbs (113 kg) | 225 | ~295 |

Running 1.5 miles burns about **30% more than walking it**, even though it takes half the time — running is roughly 9.8 METs against 3.5 for a moderate walk. But the walk still wins on total steps by about 1,000, so which one "counts for more" depends entirely on whether you're chasing a calorie target or a step target. [Calories burned walking 10 km](/blog/calories-burned-walking-10km) scales the same math to a much longer distance.

Add hills and the picture changes again. A 5% incline pushes calorie burn up 40–50% and shortens your stride, so the same 1.5 miles costs both more energy and more steps — closer to 3,700–3,900 for an average adult.

## What 1.5 Miles Looks Like in a Real Week

Three 1.5-mile walks a day is 4.5 miles and roughly 10,400 steps — the classic 10,000-step target, assembled entirely out of half-hour pieces with no gym trip involved. Two a day is about 6,900 steps, which lands most people in the range health researchers associate with meaningful reductions in all-cause mortality.

If your aim is weight loss rather than a fitness-test time, distance-per-week matters more than any single walk. [How much should I walk to lose weight](/blog/how-much-should-i-walk-to-lose-weight) works through the weekly numbers.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How many steps is 1.5 miles walking?

About **3,450 steps** for an average adult at a normal 3 mph pace. The realistic range is 3,000 to 3,850 steps: a 5'0" adult takes roughly 3,830, while a 6'4" adult takes about 3,025. Walking briskly reduces the count by 200–300 steps.

### How many steps is 1.5 miles running?

About **2,400 steps** for an average adult, roughly 30% fewer than walking the same distance. Running step length is around 60% of your height versus about 42% when walking, so each step covers far more ground. See [how many steps in a mile running](/blog/how-many-steps-in-a-mile-running) for the per-mile breakdown.

### Is 1.5 miles a good daily walk?

Yes, for most people it's a solid daily baseline. It's about 3,450 steps, 30 minutes, and 130–180 calories — enough to satisfy one day of the CDC's 150-minutes-per-week moderate activity guideline if you walk it briskly. If you're already active, treat it as a floor rather than a target.

### How many steps is 1.5 miles on a treadmill?

Essentially the same as outdoors: **3,300–3,500 steps** at a normal walking pace. Treadmill belts can slightly shorten stride at low speeds, and adding incline shortens it further, so a 1.5-mile treadmill walk at 5% grade may register closer to 3,700 steps. Treadmill consoles report distance from belt speed, not from your stride, so they won't always match your phone's step-derived distance.

### How long is 1.5 miles in km?

1.5 miles is **2.41 kilometers** (2,414 meters). That's six laps of a standard 400 m outdoor track plus about 14 meters — which is why most 1.5-mile fitness tests are run as a flat six laps.

### How many steps is half of 1.5 miles?

0.75 miles is about **1,725 steps** for an average adult, or 15 minutes of walking. For the shorter benchmarks, see [how many steps is half a mile](/blog/how-many-steps-is-half-a-mile).

## The Bottom Line

1.5 miles is about **3,450 steps and 30 minutes** walking, or **2,400 steps and 12–18 minutes** running. Shorter adults should expect closer to 3,800 walking; taller adults closer to 3,100.

It's a distance worth knowing precisely, because it doubles as a fitness benchmark. The Navy, Air Force, Coast Guard, and most police and fire academies all measure aerobic capacity over exactly this stretch — and the number of steps it takes you to cover it is a direct readout of your stride and cadence.
