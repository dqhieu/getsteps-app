# Walking vs Swimming: Which Burns More Calories and Is Better for You?

**In the walking vs swimming debate, swimming burns more calories per minute at lap intensity, while walking is simpler, weight-bearing (better for bone density), and easier to sustain daily.** A 155-lb person burns roughly 290 calories in 30 minutes of moderate freestyle versus about 175 calories brisk walking — but when effort is matched, fat loss is similar, and walking wins on convenience, bone load, and consistency. Both are excellent cardio; pick the one you'll actually do.

This guide compares walking vs swimming on calories, weight loss, joints, bone density, heart health, convenience, and step equivalents, with calorie tables and a winner column.

## Walking vs Swimming: Calories Burned Compared

People search walking vs swimming mostly for calories. Burn depends on intensity, duration, and body weight. Compare using **MET** (Metabolic Equivalent of Task) from the Compendium of Physical Activities — higher MET means more calories per minute.

| Activity | Pace / Stroke | MET | Intensity |
|----------|---------------|-----|-----------|
| Walking | 2.5 mph (4 km/h), leisure | 3.0 | Light-moderate |
| Walking | 3.0 mph (4.8 km/h) | 3.5 | Moderate |
| Walking | 4.0 mph (6.4 km/h), brisk | 5.0 | Moderate |
| Swimming | Leisurely, not laps | 6.0 | Moderate |
| Swimming | Freestyle, moderate (~50 yd/min) | 8.3 | Vigorous |
| Swimming | Freestyle, vigorous | 9.8 | Vigorous |

**Leisurely swimming (6.0 MET) and brisk walking (5.0 MET) are close.** Swimming only pulls ahead once you swim genuine laps — not floating or chatting in the shallow end.

## Walking vs Swimming for Weight Loss

Both work. A calorie deficit does the fat loss; the activity only has to be repeatable.

**What the trials show:**

- **Gappmaier et al. (2006)** compared water exercise with land walking in obese women at matched intensity. Fat loss was essentially the same. Intensity — not water versus pavement — drove the result.
- **SWEAT 2** (Cox et al.) followed previously sedentary women for 12 months. Swimming produced a slightly larger weight and lipid benefit (~1.1 kg more). Real, not a knockout.
- **Gwinup (1987)** is still quoted: walkers lost weight, swimmers lost none. The sample was small, diet was uncontrolled, and later trials did not show swimming "cannot" cut fat. Cold water can raise appetite — behavior, not a metabolic dead end.

Swimming's edge is calories per minute at lap pace, plus full-body work when joints hurt. Walking's edge is consistency — a [two-hour daily walk](/blog/if-i-walk-2-hours-a-day-how-much-weight-will-i-lose) or a stretch of [power walking](/blog/power-walking-benefits) closes much of the gap without a pool, and the load also builds bone.

The honest answer to walking vs swimming for fat loss: **swimming wins calories-per-hour once you swim laps; walking wins consistency.** Matched effort, similar fat loss. Set a target with the [weight loss walking calculator](/tools/weight-loss-walking-calculator).

## Joint Impact: Is Swimming Easier on the Body Than Walking?

Both are **low-impact** compared with running; they load joints differently.

- **Swimming is non-weight-bearing.** Buoyancy unloads most of your body weight — which is why it is prescribed for **osteoarthritis, rheumatoid flares, back pain, and post-surgery rehab**.
- **Walking is weight-bearing and mostly gentle.** Each footfall is typically about 1–1.5× body weight. That mild load moves synovial fluid and maintains joint nutrition. During a significant flare, walking can aggravate symptoms where water will not.

If joints currently hurt, swim. If they are healthy and you want the bone bonus, walk.

## Bone Density: Walking Wins Because It Is Weight-Bearing

This is the clearest split in walking vs swimming.

Bone remodels under mechanical load. Walking provides that load every step. Swimming removes it. Aquatic athletes often show **lower bone mineral density** than walkers, runners, or people who lift — not because swimming is harmful, but because water does not stress the skeleton.

If osteoporosis prevention or [increasing bone density](/blog/does-walking-increase-bone-density) is a goal, make walking plus two short strength sessions the primary habit. Keep swimming for cardio and joints; do not make it your only stimulus if bone matters.

## Heart Health, Muscle, Convenience, and Cost

**Heart.** Both count toward 150 minutes a week of moderate aerobic work. Brisk walking is enough cardio for most longevity goals — see [is walking enough exercise](/blog/is-walking-enough-exercise). Swimming scales intensity easily; water pressure can aid venous return. The winner is whichever you keep doing.

**Muscle.** Walking trains legs and a quiet core. Swimming adds lats, shoulders, and chest. More muscle per minute: swim. A habit you will not skip: walk.

**Convenience and cost.** Walking needs shoes. Swimming needs a pool, a slot, and often a membership. You can walk immediately; you cannot sneak ten minutes of freestyle between meetings. Steps still stack — a [3,000-step](/blog/is-3000-steps-a-day-enough) floor or [20,000-step](/blog/is-20000-steps-a-day-good) days — without driving to a facility.

## Which Is Better for You: Walking vs Swimming?

No universal winner — a best fit for *your* situation:

- **Painful joints, arthritis, or rehab?** → **Swimming.** Lowest joint load.
- **Build or keep bone density?** → **Walking.** Weight-bearing load.
- **Short on time, want max burn?** → **Swimming** — if you can get to a pool and swim real laps.
- **Beginner or very deconditioned?** → **Walking.** Zero technique, zero equipment.
- **Struggle with consistency?** → **Walking.**
- **Want full-body cardio?** → **Swimming.**
- **Tight budget or no pool?** → **Walking.**
- **Evening wind-down / sleep?** → A walk is easier to slot in; see [does walking help you sleep](/blog/does-walking-help-you-sleep).
- **Sore from a hard gym day?** → Easy swim or easy walk.

## Can You Combine Walking and Swimming?

Yes — and often the smartest plan.

- **Swim** when joints feel cranky or you want a full-body, time-efficient session.
- **Walk** the other days for bone load, steps, and all-day movement.
- **Alternate** to spare shoulders from high swim volume and to avoid boredom.

A realistic week: 2 swim sessions plus walking on the other days, including incidental steps.

## Common Walking vs Swimming Questions

### Is walking or swimming better for weight loss?

Both work in a calorie deficit. Swimming burns more per minute at lap intensity; walking is easier to repeat daily. Matched-intensity trials (Gappmaier 2006) show similar fat loss. SWEAT 2 found a small swimming edge at 12 months (~1.1 kg). The better tool is the one you will still do in six months.

### Which burns more calories, walking or swimming?

At typical lap effort, swimming burns more — about 60–65% more in 30 minutes versus brisk walking (roughly 290 vs 175 calories for a 155-lb person). Leisurely swimming (6.0 MET) can land close to brisk walking (5.0 MET). Pace matters more than the label.

### Is swimming better for your joints than walking?

Yes if you currently have joint pain. Swimming is non-weight-bearing; buoyancy unloads knees, hips, and spine. Walking's mild load is beneficial for healthy joints and essential for bone, but it can aggravate a flare.

### Does swimming count as steps?

Not as literal steps. Convert it: 30 minutes of moderate swimming is about **6,000 equivalent steps**. Log it in Steps or use the [activity to steps converter](/tools/activity-to-steps-converter).

## The Verdict on Walking vs Swimming

Walking vs swimming is a toolkit, not a fight. Swimming delivers more calories per minute at lap intensity, near-zero joint impact, and full-body work. Walking delivers convenience, bone-building load, near-zero cost, and easier consistency. Match the activity to your joints, pool access, and goals — or use both.
