# How Much Should I Walk to Lose Weight? Minutes Per Day (2026)

**If you're asking how much should I walk to lose weight, the answer in minutes is 45 to 60 minutes a day, six days a week — about 270 to 360 minutes weekly. The CDC's 150 minutes a week is the health floor, not the weight-loss dose. Research bodies put the fat-loss threshold closer to 300 minutes a week.**

Most guides answer this question in steps or miles. But "how much should I walk" is a question about **time** — how many minutes you need to carve out of a day. That's the number that decides whether the plan survives contact with your calendar. Here it is, in minutes, with the calorie math attached.

## The Two Thresholds: 150 vs 300 Minutes a Week

There are two different numbers in circulation and they answer two different questions.

**150 minutes of moderate activity per week** is the [CDC's Physical Activity Guidelines for Americans](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/guidelines/adults.html) minimum. It is a *health* target — it lowers cardiovascular risk, blood pressure, and diabetes risk. It was never a weight-loss prescription. At 150 minutes of brisk walking a week, a 180 lb person burns about 920 calories — roughly a quarter pound of fat.

**300 minutes a week** is where weight change starts showing up reliably. The American College of Sports Medicine puts the useful range at **150–250 minutes per week for modest loss and more than 250 minutes per week for meaningful weight loss and for preventing regain**. The CDC guidelines echo this, describing 300+ minutes weekly as the level associated with additional health and weight benefits.

So the honest answer to how much you should walk to lose weight is: **more than the number you've probably heard.** 30 minutes a day, five days a week gets you healthier. 45–60 minutes a day, six days a week is what moves the scale.

| Weekly minutes | Daily equivalent | What it's for |
|---|---|---|
| 150 min | 30 min × 5 days | CDC health minimum — not a weight-loss dose |
| 200–250 min | 35–40 min × 6 days | Modest loss, meaningful for beginners |
| 300 min | 50 min × 6 days | The weight-loss threshold |
| 350–400 min | 60 min × 6 days | Faster loss, regain prevention |

## How Much Should I Walk to Lose Weight? The Full Table

This is the table that answers the question. All figures assume **brisk walking at 3.5 mph** (about 4.3 METs — the pace where you can talk but not sing) and **6 walking days a week**. Monthly loss assumes the walking is added on top of an unchanged diet.

| Minutes/day | Weekly minutes | 150 lb | 180 lb | 220 lb |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 15 min | 90 min | 460 cal → 0.6 lb/mo | 550 cal → 0.7 lb/mo | 675 cal → 0.8 lb/mo |
| 30 min | 180 min | 920 cal → 1.1 lb/mo | 1,100 cal → 1.4 lb/mo | 1,350 cal → 1.7 lb/mo |
| 45 min | 270 min | 1,380 cal → 1.7 lb/mo | 1,650 cal → 2.0 lb/mo | 2,025 cal → 2.5 lb/mo |
| 60 min | 360 min | 1,840 cal → 2.3 lb/mo | 2,200 cal → 2.7 lb/mo | 2,700 cal → 3.3 lb/mo |
| 90 min | 540 min | 2,750 cal → 3.4 lb/mo | 3,300 cal → 4.1 lb/mo | 4,050 cal → 5.0 lb/mo |

Three things jump out of this table.

**Fifteen minutes a day is not a weight-loss intervention.** It's a good habit and a fine on-ramp, but at under a pound a month it will not feel like it's working. If that's all the time you have, pair it with a diet change rather than expecting the walk to carry the result.

**Body weight does a lot of the work.** A 220 lb person walking 45 minutes loses about as much per month as a 150 lb person walking 60. Heavier bodies burn more per minute, which is why walking often produces fast early results and then slows as you get lighter.

**The jump from 30 to 60 minutes roughly doubles the outcome** — but so does the time cost. Run your own numbers with the [Weight Loss Walking Calculator](/tools/weight-loss-walking-calculator), which lets you set your weight, pace, and target loss rate instead of using the averages above.

## Why the Table Overpromises (And What to Do About It)

Every calorie table on the internet, including the one above, assumes your body cooperates. It usually doesn't, and this is the part most articles skip.

Two things eat into the predicted loss:

**Compensatory eating.** Appetite rises after exercise, and an hour of walking is easy to erase with a 300-calorie snack. Studies of exercise-only weight loss consistently find people lose **less than half** of what the arithmetic predicts, and the gap is almost entirely food.

**Metabolic adaptation.** Your body gets more efficient at repeated movement, and non-exercise activity often drops on days you train — you sit more, fidget less.

The fix isn't more minutes: **treat the walk as the deficit, not the permission.** If your walking minutes are consistent for four weeks and the scale hasn't moved, the problem is intake, not volume. The [Calorie Deficit Calculator](/tools/calorie-deficit-calculator) shows what your maintenance number actually is.

Expect to realize roughly **60–70% of the table's predicted loss** unless you're also tracking food. That still puts an hour a day at a real 1.5–2 lb a month, which compounds into 20+ pounds a year.

## How to Structure the Minutes

Total weekly minutes is what matters. How you slice them is up to you, and the flexibility is the whole advantage of a time-based target.

**Split sessions count fully.** Three 20-minute walks produce the same energy expenditure as one 60-minute walk, and adherence is measurably better. A 2025 trial in *Obesity* found split walking sessions produced equal or better fat-loss outcomes than a single continuous session in adults with overweight.

**Pace sets the floor.** The minutes only count if the walking is brisk — roughly 3 to 4 mph, about 100–120 steps per minute, breathing deeper than at rest. A stroll at 2 mph burns nearly 40% less per minute, which means a "60-minute walk" at strolling pace is really a 37-minute walk in calorie terms. See [what is brisk walking](/blog/what-is-brisk-walking) for how to check your pace, and the [Walking Calories Calculator](/tools/walking-calories-calculator) to see exactly what your pace is worth.

**Bank the week, not the day.** Missing Tuesday doesn't matter if Saturday is 90 minutes. Aim at the weekly number — 270 or 360 — and let individual days move around.

**Add hills before adding time.** A 5% incline raises calorie burn 30–50% at the same pace and duration. It's the cheapest way to get more out of minutes you're already spending.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much should I walk to lose weight if I only have 30 minutes a day?

Thirty minutes of brisk walking, six days a week, burns roughly 900–1,350 calories weekly depending on your weight — about 1 to 1.7 pounds a month on its own. That's real but slow. To speed it up without adding time, walk faster or add an incline, and pair it with a 250-calorie daily diet reduction, which roughly doubles the result.

### Is 150 minutes of walking a week enough to lose weight?

Not usually. 150 minutes a week is the CDC minimum for **health**, not weight loss. At that volume most people lose under a pound a month from walking alone. The American College of Sports Medicine points to more than 250 minutes a week for meaningful weight loss and for preventing regain after you've lost it.

### How long should I walk each day to lose 1 pound a week?

One pound a week requires a 3,500-calorie weekly deficit. Walking alone: a 180 lb person would need roughly **95 minutes a day, six days a week** at a brisk pace. That's unrealistic for most people, which is why the standard approach is 45–60 minutes of walking plus a modest calorie reduction — the walking covers about half the deficit and food covers the rest.

### Is it better to walk longer or faster to lose weight?

Longer wins on total calories; faster wins on calories per minute. If time is your constraint, increase pace — going from 3.0 to 4.0 mph burns about 45% more per minute. If time is available, add minutes, because a longer moderate walk is easier to sustain and far less likely to cause overuse injuries.

### How soon will I see results from walking 45–60 minutes a day?

Expect measurable scale change in **4 to 6 weeks**, and visible change closer to 8–12. The first two weeks often show a fast drop (water and glycogen) followed by a plateau — that plateau is normal and not a failure of the plan. Judge progress on a four-week rolling average, not week to week.

## The Bottom Line

How much should you walk to lose weight? **45 to 60 minutes a day, six days a week — 270 to 360 minutes weekly.** The 150-minute figure everyone quotes is a health minimum that was never designed to produce weight loss.

At that volume, expect 1.7 to 3.3 pounds a month from the walking alone, less if you're eating back the deficit. Keep the pace brisk, count the week rather than the day, and add hills before you add hours.

**This article is for general information and is not medical advice.** Talk to your doctor before starting a new exercise program, especially if you have joint problems, heart conditions, or a significant amount of weight to lose.
