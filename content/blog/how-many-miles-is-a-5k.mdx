# How Many Miles Is a 5K?

**How many miles is a 5K?** A 5K is exactly **3.1 miles** (4.97 km). The "5K" simply means 5 kilometers, and since one kilometer equals about 0.621 miles, multiplying 5 by 0.621 gives you 3.107 miles — which everyone rounds to 3.1. It is the most popular race distance in the world and the perfect first goal for new walkers and runners.

If you have ever signed up for a fun run, a charity walk, or your first organized race, odds are it was a 5K. Knowing how many miles is a 5K helps you plan your training, estimate your finish time, and understand exactly how far you will travel. In this guide we cover the 5K conversion, how it stacks up against the 10K, half marathon, and marathon, how long it takes to walk or run, and how many steps a 5K adds to your day.

## Quick Answer: How Many Miles Is a 5K?

Here is the short version:

- **5K = 3.1 miles**
- **5K = 5 kilometers**
- **5K = 4.97 km** (5,000 meters, rounded to 3.1 miles)
- **5K = roughly 6,600 steps walking** or **4,700 steps running**

So when someone asks how many miles is a 5K, the answer is 3.1 miles — a distance most people can walk in under an hour. To convert any kilometer figure to miles yourself, just multiply by 0.621. To go the other way (miles to km), multiply by 1.609.

## Race Distance Conversion Table: 5K, 10K, Half & Marathon

A 5K is just the start. Here is how the most common race distances convert into miles, kilometers, and approximate walking step counts (based on an average adult stride of about 0.75 m / 2.5 ft):

| Race | Kilometers | Miles | Approx. Walking Steps |
|------|-----------|-------|----------------------|
| 5K | 5 km | 3.1 mi | ~6,600 |
| 10K | 10 km | 6.2 mi | ~13,200 |
| 15K | 15 km | 9.3 mi | ~19,800 |
| Half Marathon | 21.1 km | 13.1 mi | ~27,800 |
| Marathon | 42.2 km | 26.2 mi | ~55,600 |

A few things stand out. A **10K is exactly double a 5K** — 6.2 miles. A **half marathon is 13.1 miles**, more than four times a 5K, and a **full marathon is 26.2 miles**, which is eight 5Ks back to back. If you are working up to longer races, mastering the 5K first builds the foundation.

The step counts above are for walking. Runners cover more ground per step, so running these distances takes roughly 30% fewer steps. For a deeper look at the longer end, see [how many steps in a half marathon](/blog/how-many-steps-in-a-half-marathon).

## How Many Miles Is a 5K Compared to Familiar Distances

Sometimes "3.1 miles" is easier to picture with everyday comparisons:

- About **12 laps** around a standard outdoor running track (each lap is 400 m)
- Roughly the distance of **62 American football fields** end to end
- A typical **brisk 45-minute walk** for most adults
- Around **3 miles** of city blocks (about 60 blocks in a standard grid)

Understanding how many miles is a 5K in concrete terms makes the distance feel achievable. Most people already walk close to a 5K over the course of a normal busy day without realizing it.

## How Long Does It Take to Walk or Run a 5K?

Now that you know how many miles is a 5K, the next question is how long it takes to cover those 3.1 miles. The answer depends entirely on your pace. Here are typical 5K finish times:

| Pace Type | Speed | 5K Finish Time |
|-----------|-------|----------------|
| Casual walk | 2.5 mph | ~74 minutes |
| Moderate walk | 3.0 mph | ~62 minutes |
| Brisk walk | 3.5 mph | ~53 minutes |
| Power walk | 4.0 mph | ~47 minutes |
| Beginner jog | 5.0 mph | ~37 minutes |
| Intermediate run | 6.0 mph | ~31 minutes |
| Advanced run | 7.5 mph | ~25 minutes |
| Elite run | 9.0+ mph | ~20 minutes or less |

Most recreational runners finish a 5K in **25 to 35 minutes**. Walkers usually take **45 to 75 minutes** depending on pace and terrain. There is no wrong speed — finishing the distance is the win.

## How Many Steps Is a 5K?

Since a 5K is 3.1 miles, it lands at a predictable number of steps. For an average adult (5'7" / 170 cm):

- **Walking a 5K:** ~6,600 steps
- **Brisk walking a 5K:** ~6,100 steps
- **Running a 5K:** ~4,700 steps

The difference comes down to stride length. Walkers average about 0.75 m per step, while runners stretch to 1.0–1.2 m, covering 3.1 miles in fewer total strides. Your personal count depends on your height — shorter walkers may take 7,800 steps, while taller walkers might need only 5,700.

A single walking 5K covers about two-thirds of a 10,000-step day, so it is one of the most efficient ways to hit your target. ## Why the 5K Is the Perfect Starting Distance

Once you know how many miles is a 5K — just 3.1 — it is easy to see why it dominates the racing calendar. The distance is long enough to feel like an accomplishment but short enough that almost anyone can train for it in a few weeks.

**It is beginner-friendly.** You do not need months of preparation. A complete beginner can walk a 5K today and build toward running one in 8–9 weeks with a Couch to 5K plan.

**It is low-commitment.** Training requires only 20–40 minutes a few times per week, which fits around work and family.

**It is everywhere.** Parkruns, charity 5Ks, and community fun runs happen weekly in nearly every city, so there is always a race to sign up for.

**It scales up.** Once you can comfortably run 3.1 miles, the 10K (6.2 miles) is the natural next step, followed by the half marathon and marathon.

## How Many Calories Does a 5K Burn?

Covering 3.1 miles burns a meaningful number of calories. The exact figure depends on your weight and whether you walk or run:

| Body Weight | Walking a 5K | Running a 5K |
|-------------|--------------|--------------|
| 120 lb (54 kg) | ~200 cal | ~260 cal |
| 150 lb (68 kg) | ~250 cal | ~320 cal |
| 180 lb (82 kg) | ~310 cal | ~395 cal |
| 220 lb (100 kg) | ~380 cal | ~485 cal |

Running burns more per minute, but because walking the same 3.1 miles takes longer, the total calorie gap is smaller than most people expect. Both are excellent for fitness and weight management.

## Training Tips to Cover Your First 5K

Whether you plan to walk or run your 3.1 miles, these tips will get you to the finish line:

1. **Start where you are.** If you can walk for 20 minutes, you can begin training today. Add 5 minutes each week until you reach 45–60 minutes.
2. **Try the walk-run method.** Alternate 1 minute of jogging with 2 minutes of walking, then gradually shift toward more running.
3. **Aim for 3 sessions per week.** Consistency beats intensity. Rest days let your body adapt and prevent injury.
4. **Track your distance.** Knowing how many miles is a 5K means little if you cannot measure your own. A pedometer app shows your real-time distance so you know when you have hit 3.1 miles.
5. **Build a step habit.** Set a daily target with the [Daily Step Goal Calculator](/tools/daily-step-goal-calculator) and let everyday walking support your 5K base.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How many miles is a 5K?

A 5K is **3.1 miles** (4.97 km). The "K" stands for kilometers, so a 5K is 5 kilometers. Since one kilometer equals about 0.621 miles, 5 kilometers works out to 3.107 miles, which is universally rounded to 3.1 miles.

### Is a 5K 3 miles?

Almost — a 5K is **3.1 miles**, slightly longer than 3 miles. The extra 0.1 mile (about 177 yards) comes from the conversion between kilometers and miles. If you train by running exactly 3 miles, you will be about 530 feet short of a true 5K.

### How long does it take to walk a 5K?

Most people walk a 5K in **45 to 75 minutes**. At a moderate 3.0 mph pace it takes about 62 minutes, while a brisk 3.5–4.0 mph pace brings it down to roughly 47–53 minutes. Your time depends on pace, terrain, and fitness level.

### How many steps is a 5K?

Walking a 5K is about **6,600 steps** for an average adult, while running it is closer to **4,700 steps**. The exact number depends on your height and stride length — taller people take fewer steps, shorter people take more.

### How many miles is a 10K, half marathon, and marathon?

A **10K is 6.2 miles**, a **half marathon is 13.1 miles** (21.1 km), and a **full marathon is 26.2 miles** (42.2 km). A 10K is exactly double a 5K, and a marathon equals eight 5Ks back to back.

### Is a 5K good exercise?

Yes. Covering 3.1 miles — whether walking or running — counts as moderate-to-vigorous exercise, burns 200–485 calories depending on your weight and pace, and contributes most of a 10,000-step day. It is one of the most accessible and rewarding fitness goals you can set.
