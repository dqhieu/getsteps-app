# Hot Girl Walk: What It Is and How to Start

A **hot girl walk** is a planned outdoor walk, often about 4 miles, with three subjects to think about: what you are grateful for, your goals and how you will work toward them, and your own confidence. Mia Lind started the practice in 2020, while she was a student at USC, as a way to manage stress. She posted it on TikTok in January 2021, and it spread widely from there. The phrase is associated with her brand. What follows is the practice itself: distance, time, steps, and a smaller way to begin.

## What a Hot Girl Walk Is

The usual version is an outdoor walk of about 4 miles. Some descriptions say 3–4 miles. You pick a route, walk at a comfortable pace, and spend the time on three things, in any order.

The first is gratitude: specific things from ordinary life. The second is your goals, including the next step you will actually take. The third is confidence. Lind's phrasing for that third one is self-regard ("how hot you are"). She means confidence, in the sense of regard for yourself, separate from appearance or weight.

It is a mindset walk. It is not a weight-loss plan, and it is not a substitute for strength training. The research on mood, stress, and depression risk is in the [mental benefits of walking](/blog/mental-benefits-of-walking).

Anyone can do the walk. The trend's name is gendered. The habit, a planned outdoor walk plus three prompts, is not limited to one group.

## How Long a Hot Girl Walk Takes

Four miles is the distance people cite. At a typical walk, that is about **8,000–10,000 steps** and about **60–80 minutes**.

The step count uses a common range of 2,000–2,400 steps per mile. Four times that is 8,000–9,600 steps. Shorter strides can land nearer 10,000, which is why people often say 8,000–10,000. The [steps per mile calculator](/tools/steps-per-mile-calculator) estimates your own number, and [how many steps in a mile](/blog/how-many-steps-in-a-mile) explains those per-mile figures.

Time is simpler, because it follows pace:

- At 20 minutes per mile (3 mph), 4 miles takes 80 minutes.
- At 15 minutes per mile (4 mph), 4 miles takes 60 minutes.

| Distance | Approximate steps | Time at 3 mph | Time at 4 mph |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 mile | 2,000–2,400 | 20 min | 15 min |
| 2 miles | 4,000–4,800 | 40 min | 30 min |
| 3 miles | 6,000–7,200 | 60 min | 45 min |
| 4 miles | 8,000–9,600 | 80 min | 60 min |

The table uses 2,000–2,400 steps per mile, so the 4-mile row stops at 9,600. Treat "about 10,000" as the upper end for a shorter stride. If your route is not a round number of miles, the [walking time calculator](/tools/walking-time-calculator) converts distance and pace into minutes.

Three miles per hour is an ordinary pace. Four miles per hour is brisk, and the prompts do not require it. If you are too out of breath to think in sentences, slow down.

A single 4-mile outing is a large block of a day. [Walking 4 miles a day](/blog/walking-4-miles-a-day) looks at that distance as a standing habit, apart from this trend. You can also split the time. The prompts do not require one continuous loop.

## What to think about while you walk

You do not need a script. One pass through each prompt is enough.

**Gratitude.** Name a few concrete things from this week: a person who helped, a meal, a quiet stretch of the route, a task you finished. A specific scene is easier to stay with than a general mood.

**Goals.** Choose one or two you are already working on, then stay with the next action. A fitness goal might mean Thursday's route. A work goal might mean the first task tomorrow morning.

**Confidence.** This is regard for yourself: what you have already handled, and how you want to meet the rest of the day. It is not a check of your body, and it is not a number on a scale.

Some people give each prompt a portion of the route and leave the last stretch quiet. Others stay with whichever thought is actually present. The structure gives the walk a subject so the time does not default to the first worry that showed up.

If mornings are when you can leave the house, that slot is a practical one. [Morning walk benefits](/blog/morning-walk-benefits) covers light, timing, and why a walk before the rest of the day is easier to keep.

## A rough calorie estimate

Calorie burn is not the point of the practice. If you still want a figure, a moderate walk is often modeled at about 3.5 METs. At that intensity, 4 miles is roughly 40% of a 10-mile moderate walk. Estimated burn for the 4 miles:

- about 260 kcal at 125 lb
- about 330 kcal at 155 lb
- about 390 kcal at 185 lb
- about 450 kcal at 215 lb

These are estimates. Hills, stops, pace, and how much you carry all move the result. The [walking calories calculator](/tools/walking-calories-calculator) takes your weight and pace. For the longer walk those percentages refer to, see [walking 10 miles a day](/blog/walking-10-miles-a-day).

A steady 60- to 80-minute walk can count as moderate cardio. It still does not stand in for strength training. [Is walking enough exercise](/blog/is-walking-enough-exercise) separates what walking covers from what it leaves out.

## How to Do a Hot Girl Walk This Week

Start at 20 minutes, not at 4 miles. Lind has said the distance is a benchmark, not a rule. CBS reported her suggestion that three 20-minute walks can add up to about 3 miles, and that people should start smaller rather than forcing 4 miles.

A first week can stay that small:

1. Choose three days. Put 20 minutes on the calendar, outside, at a time you can actually leave.
2. Use a loop you already know, in daylight, where other people are around.
3. Walk. Touch each prompt once. A minute on one of them still counts.
4. If 20 minutes feels easy by the third outing, add about 10 minutes the following week.
5. Only later, if you want the benchmark, build toward 4 miles. At 3 mph that is 80 minutes, and it does not need to happen in week one.

Three 20-minute walks are an hour outside. At a typical pace that is on the order of 3 miles across the week, which is the split CBS described. That is enough to learn whether the prompts are useful before you commit to a long route. You can also attach the prompts to a walk you were already going to take.

## Staying safe on the route

Walk where you can be seen. If you go alone, tell someone your route and roughly when you expect to be back. If you wear headphones, keep one ear free. Change routes so you are not on the same path at the same hour every time. Daylight is easier: you can see the ground, and other people can see you.

These are ordinary precautions for walking outside. They matter more on a long loop than on a 20-minute circuit of your block.

## Common Questions

### What is a hot girl walk?

A hot girl walk is an intentional outdoor walk, often about 4 miles, focused on gratitude, your goals and how you will work toward them, and your own confidence. Mia Lind began it in 2020 to manage stress and shared it on TikTok in January 2021. The phrase is associated with her brand. The practice is the walk and the three prompts.

### How many steps is a hot girl walk?

About 8,000–10,000 steps for 4 miles, using roughly 2,000–2,400 steps per mile. A 20-minute start is closer to 1 mile for many people, or about 2,000–2,400 steps, depending on stride and pace. The table above scales that same range to 2, 3, and 4 miles.

### Do you need 4 miles?

No. Four miles is the usual benchmark, not a requirement. A 20-minute walk with the same three prompts is the same practice at a smaller dose. Three of those walks can add up to about 3 miles.

### Is a hot girl walk a workout?

It can be moderate cardio when the walk is long enough and the pace is steady. Sixty to eighty minutes is a real session. Twenty minutes is still worth doing, and it is still not strength training. Keep it as a walk with a mental script. Add separate strength work if strength is one of your goals.
