# Is 12,000 Steps a Day Good? What Research Says

**Yes. If you can hold 12,000 steps a day without pain, it is a good target.** It is an active day, not the minimum useful dose, and not dangerous compared with 8,000. A 2020 *JAMA* study of US adults found a lower mortality hazard at 12,000 steps than at 8,000, both versus 4,000. A 2025 *Lancet Public Health* dose-response review still put most of the gain earlier: about 7,000 steps already carries a large share, and the authors leave 10,000 for people who are already more active. More steps were not associated with higher risk. The extra benefit gets smaller.

So if you are asking **is 12000 steps a day good**, you are past the steep part of the curve. Seven thousand is already meaningful. Ten thousand is optional. Twelve thousand is a solid active day when you can repeat it.

Below: what 12,000 steps looks like, what those papers measured, and how to add steps a week or two at a time.

## What 12,000 Steps Looks Like in a Real Day

On this site, **12,000 steps** is about **5 to 6 miles**: 12,000 divided by 2,400 steps per mile at the short-stride end, and 12,000 divided by 2,000 at the long-stride end. Five miles is about 8 km and six miles is about 9.7 km, so call it roughly **8 to 10 km**. Height and stride move you inside that band. The full table is in [how many miles is 12,000 steps](/blog/how-many-miles-is-12000-steps), and a personal figure is in the [Step Distance Calculator](/tools/step-distance-calculator).

| | 12,000 steps |
|---|---|
| Miles | About 5–6 |
| Kilometers | Roughly 8–10 km |
| Time at 100 steps per minute of walking | 120 minutes |
| Scheduled walks | Often under 2 hours |
| Who it fits | People already near 8,000–10,000 who feel fine |

The 120 minutes assumes every step is continuous walking at 100 per minute. Real days mix short indoor steps with longer walks, so the clock time of walking workouts is often less than 2 hours.

Counts of 10,000 and above sit in the usual active band: [how many steps a day is considered active](/blog/how-many-steps-a-day-is-considered-active). Ten miles is a different project, close to double this distance: [walking 10 miles a day](/blog/walking-10-miles-a-day).

## What the Research Says About 12,000 Steps

**Saint-Maurice and colleagues, *JAMA*, 2020 (US adults).** Compared with 4,000 steps a day, 8,000 steps had a hazard ratio of about **0.49** for all-cause mortality, and 12,000 steps had a hazard ratio of about **0.35**. After adjusting for total steps, step intensity was not significantly related to mortality. The count carried the association.

**Paluch and the Steps for Health Collaborative.** In older adults, a lot of the benefit showed up around **6,000–8,000 steps**. In adults under 60, around **8,000–10,000**. Someone at 12,000 is not at higher risk than someone at 8,000. Twelve thousand sits a little above the under-60 band and well above the older-adult band. Nearby targets on the same curve: [is 8,000 steps a day good](/blog/is-8000-steps-a-day-good), [is 9,000 steps a day good](/blog/is-9000-steps-a-day-good), and [is 14,000 steps a day good](/blog/is-14000-steps-a-day-good).

**Lancet Public Health, 2025 (dose-response meta-analysis).** Compared with 2,000 steps a day, about 7,000 steps a day was associated with:

- **47% lower** all-cause mortality (HR 0.53)
- **25% lower** cardiovascular disease incidence
- **47% lower** cardiovascular mortality
- **37% lower** cancer mortality
- **14% lower** type 2 diabetes incidence
- **38% lower** dementia
- **22% lower** depressive symptoms
- **28% lower** falls

Several outcomes bent around **5,000–7,000 steps**. The authors say 10,000 can still suit people who are already more active, and that 7,000 may be a more realistic target. More steps were not associated with higher risk. The extra benefit gets smaller. Detail on the earlier target: [is 7,000 steps a day enough](/blog/is-7000-steps-a-day-enough). The round-number case: [10,000 steps a day benefits](/blog/10000-steps-a-day-benefits).

**Chiang and colleagues, *BMC Public Health*, 2019.** An 8-week randomized trial in young adults with obesity. A daily goal around 12,000 steps without a pace rule did not improve body composition. HDL cholesterol did rise in that step-goal group. The group that also walked at more than **103 steps per minute** on 3 days a week improved hip circumference, visceral fat, HDL, fasting glucose, and triglycerides. Step count and brisk minutes are different tools.

## How 12,000 Steps Compares With 7,000 and 10,000

| | 7,000 steps | 10,000 steps | 12,000 steps |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2025 review | Realistic target; many curves bend here | Suits people already more active | Further along; extra gain is smaller |
| Versus a low baseline | HR 0.53 for death vs 2,000 | Optional headroom | *JAMA*: HR about 0.35 vs 4,000 (8,000 was about 0.49) |
| Practical read | Meaningful dose | Optional active goal | Active day, if you can hold it |

Seven thousand already covers that 2025 list. Ten thousand is optional. Twelve thousand adds time on your feet and, in the *JAMA* cohort, a lower hazard than 8,000 against the same 4,000-step reference. If 10,000 feels easy and nothing hurts, 12,000 is a reasonable next active target.

## Is 12,000 Steps a Day Good for Weight Loss?

It can support a loss, with the Chiang limit still in force: about 12,000 steps without a pace rule did not change body composition in that 8-week trial, and the brisk arm did. Food still decides whether the week is a deficit.

## Who 12,000 Steps Fits

**Already near 8,000–10,000, with no pain.** This is the group the number is for. You are past the steep part of the curve, and more steps were not tied to higher risk than 8,000.

**Under 60.** Paluch's band runs to about 8,000–10,000. Twelve thousand is past that band. The longevity result in those cohorts does not require the extra.

**60 and older.** A lot of the benefit showed up by 6,000–8,000. Twelve thousand is reasonable when joints agree. A steady 7,000 or 8,000 already covers that range.

**Coming from 3,000–5,000.** The early gains sit on the way through 5,000 and 7,000. Treat 12,000 as a later month.

## How to Build Up to 12,000 Steps

Add roughly **500–1,000 steps a day every week or two**. Leave the one-week jump from 3,000 to 12,000 alone. Someone at 4,000 who adds about 1,000 steps every two weeks needs on the order of four months to reach 12,000.

1. Record an ordinary week first. That average is the baseline.
2. Add one 10–15 minute walk and keep it until it feels ordinary.
3. Add a second pocket: a walking call, one earlier transit stop, or a loop after a meal.
4. Raise the goal through 8,000 and 10,000 before you set 12,000.
5. If a week brings pain, hold the previous level until walking feels ordinary again.

At 100 steps per minute, a 40-minute walk is about 4,000 steps. Stack that on a day that already includes errands and 12,000 is one longer walk plus a normal day. Background steps are why the workout clock is often shorter than 120 minutes.

## Common Questions

### Is 12,000 steps a day good for your health?

Yes, when you can repeat it without pain. In *JAMA* (2020), 12,000 steps had a mortality hazard ratio of about 0.35 versus 4,000, and 8,000 steps about 0.49. The 2025 *Lancet Public Health* review finds most of the benefit by 7,000, leaves 10,000 for people already more active, and does not find higher risk as the count rises.

### Is 12,000 steps better than 8,000 or 10,000?

Against a 4,000-step reference, the *JAMA* hazards favor 12,000 over 8,000 (about 0.35 versus about 0.49). The extra slice past 7,000–10,000 is the smaller one. Going higher still: [is 14,000 steps a day good](/blog/is-14000-steps-a-day-good).

### How many miles is 12,000 steps?

About **5 to 6 miles** (roughly 8–10 km), using 2,000 to 2,400 steps per mile. See [how many miles is 12,000 steps](/blog/how-many-miles-is-12000-steps).

### How long does 12,000 steps take?

**120 minutes** if every step is walking at 100 per minute. Indoor steps mean the walks you schedule are often under two hours combined.

### Should a low step count jump to 12,000 in a week?

Add about 500–1,000 steps a day every week or two. Seven thousand is a meaningful stop on the way up: [is 7,000 steps a day enough](/blog/is-7000-steps-a-day-enough).
