**How many steps in a marathon?** Running a marathon takes roughly **38,000 steps** for an average-height adult, and walking it takes about **56,000 steps**. Most runners land somewhere between **31,000 and 46,000 steps**, and most walkers between **48,000 and 66,000**. The spread comes from stride length: height, pace, and fatigue all change how much ground each step covers.

A marathon is **26.2 miles (42.195 km)**, exactly double a [half marathon](/blog/how-many-steps-in-a-half-marathon). This guide shows how many steps are in a marathon by height, by finish time, and by cadence, plus how running compares with walking and how many calories 26.2 miles burns.

## Quick Answer: Steps in a Marathon

For an adult of average height (5'7" / 170 cm):

- **Walking a marathon (3 mph):** ~56,000 steps
- **Brisk walking a marathon (3.5 mph):** ~54,000 steps
- **Running a marathon (around a 4-hour finish):** ~38,000–40,000 steps

These numbers divide the full 42,195-meter course by a stride length. The assumptions: a walking step of about **0.75 m** (2.45 ft) and a running step of about **1.1 m** (3.6 ft). Every number in this post uses **step length**, the distance from one foot strike to the next, not a two-step stride.

That works out to about **2,150 steps per mile walking** and **1,450–1,550 steps per mile running**. If you know your own step length, the [Step Distance Calculator](/tools/step-distance-calculator) will give you a personal number.

## Steps in a Marathon by Finish Time

Height tables assume one fixed pace. Finish time tells you more. Faster runners take longer steps *and* spend fewer minutes on the course, so their totals drop quickly. The table below uses typical cadences for each pace. Steps equal cadence (steps per minute) × minutes on course:

| Finish Time | Pace per Mile | Typical Cadence | Steps in a Marathon | Implied Step |
|-------------|---------------|-----------------|---------------------|--------------|
| 2:30 (run) | 5:43 | ~180 spm | ~27,000 | 1.56 m |
| 3:00 (run) | 6:52 | ~176 spm | ~31,700 | 1.33 m |
| 3:30 (run) | 8:01 | ~172 spm | ~36,100 | 1.17 m |
| 4:00 (run) | 9:09 | ~168 spm | ~40,300 | 1.05 m |
| 4:30 (run) | 10:18 | ~164 spm | ~44,300 | 0.95 m |
| 5:00 (run) | 11:27 | ~160 spm | ~48,000 | 0.88 m |
| 6:00 (run/walk) | 13:44 | ~138 spm avg | ~49,600 | 0.85 m |
| 7:00 (fast walk, 3.7 mph) | 16:01 | ~126 spm | ~52,700 | 0.80 m |
| 7:29 (brisk walk, 3.5 mph) | 17:09 | ~120 spm | ~54,100 | 0.78 m |
| 8:44 (walk, 3 mph) | 20:00 | ~106 spm | ~55,500 | 0.76 m |

A few things stand out:

- **A 5-hour runner takes about 48,000 steps,** which is close to a fast walker. Slow running uses short steps at a high cadence.
- **The range between a 3-hour and a 5-hour runner is about 16,000 steps.** Almost all of it comes from step length, not cadence.
- **Elite marathoners** near 2:05 run at roughly 185 steps per minute with steps longer than 1.8 m. That comes to only about **23,000 steps** for the whole race.

## The Cadence Math: Count Your Own Marathon Steps

You don't need a height chart if you know two numbers. There are two ways to estimate how many steps are in a marathon for you:

**Method 1: Distance ÷ step length**

> Steps = 42,195 m ÷ your step length in meters

Example: a 1.05 m running step gives 42,195 ÷ 1.05 = **~40,200 steps**.

**Method 2: Cadence × time**

> Steps = your cadence (spm) × your finish time in minutes

Example: 168 spm for a 4:00 marathon (240 minutes) gives 168 × 240 = **40,320 steps**.

Both methods should give about the same answer, and that makes them a good cross-check. To find your step length, walk or run a measured stretch such as a 400 m track lap, count your steps, and divide 400 by the count. Your watch or phone probably already shows your cadence after a run.

**What about 180 steps per minute?** The famous "180" comes from coach Jack Daniels' counts of elite runners at the 1984 Olympics. It isn't a rule for everyone. Most recreational marathoners run at 155–175 spm. A modest cadence increase of 5% can ease impact on the knees and hips, but you don't need to hit 180 to run a good marathon.

## Running vs Walking a Marathon: Side-by-Side

| Factor | Walking a Marathon | Running a Marathon |
|--------|-------------------|-------------------|
| Typical steps | 50,000–60,000 | 32,000–46,000 |
| Steps per mile | ~2,000–2,300 | ~1,200–1,800 |
| Typical finish time | 6.5–9 hours | 3.5–5 hours |
| Step length | 0.64–0.88 m | 0.9–1.4 m |
| Cadence | 100–130 spm | 155–180 spm |
| Calories (160 lb) | ~2,200 | ~3,050 |
| Joint impact | Low | Moderate to high |

Walking a marathon takes roughly **1.5 times** as many steps as running it. Running takes half the time and burns about 35–40% more calories, but each step lands harder. Many first-timers mix the two with planned walk breaks. That puts their step count somewhere in between, usually around 45,000–52,000.

If you walk the distance, your heart rate stays low for hours, which is a big part of why walkers can go so long. Our guide to [walking heart rate](/blog/walking-heart-rate) explains which zones a long walk usually sits in.

## How a Marathon Compares to Your Daily Step Goal

A marathon packs several days of steps into one session:

- **Walking a marathon (~56,000 steps):** about **5.6 days** of a 10,000-step goal
- **Running a marathon (~38,000 steps):** about **3.8 days** of a 10,000-step goal

For scale, [walking 15,000 steps](/blog/how-long-does-it-take-to-walk-15000-steps) takes most people around two and a half hours. A walked marathon is nearly four of those back to back. A typical training week makes the gap clearer. Walking [6 miles a day](/blog/walking-6-miles-a-day) adds up to about 90,000 steps a week, which is less than two marathon walks.

## Why Your Marathon Step Count Won't Match a Chart

Over 42,195 meters, small differences add up. A step that is just 5 cm shorter adds roughly 2,000–4,000 steps over the full distance. The biggest factors:

- **Height and leg length.** The single largest influence.
- **Pace.** Faster running and walking lengthen each step.
- **Fatigue.** Steps shrink late in the race, especially past 20 miles.
- **Course and terrain.** Hills, tight turns, and crowded starts shorten steps.
- **Aid stations and stops.** Shuffling through water stops adds steps without adding much distance.
- **Tracker placement.** Wrist trackers can miss steps when your arms stop swinging, such as when you hold a cup or a phone.

So treat any marathon step count, including the ones above, as an estimate within about 5–10%.

## How Many Calories Does a Marathon Burn?

These are gross estimates and include the energy you would burn at rest anyway. Running uses the rough rule of **about 1 kcal per kg per km**. Walking assumes 3 mph at about 3.5 METs for 8.73 hours.

|-------------|-----------------------|------------------------|

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How many steps are in a marathon?

About **38,000 steps running** and **56,000 steps walking** for an adult of average height (5'7"). Most runners take 31,000–46,000 steps and most walkers take 48,000–66,000, depending on height, pace, and fatigue.

### How many steps is 26.2 miles walking?

Walking 26.2 miles takes roughly **50,000–60,000 steps** for most adults, with about **56,000** at a typical 0.75 m step. Shorter walkers can pass 65,000. Taller walkers can finish under 50,000.

### How many steps does a 4-hour marathon take?

A 4-hour marathon at about 168 steps per minute takes roughly **40,000 steps** (168 × 240 minutes). A 3:30 finish is closer to 36,000 steps, and a 5-hour finish is about 48,000.

### Do faster runners take fewer steps in a marathon?

Yes. Faster runners have longer steps and finish sooner, so they take far fewer steps. A 3-hour marathoner takes about 31,700 steps, while a 5-hour runner takes about 48,000. Elite runners need only around 23,000.

### How many steps per mile is a marathon?

About **2,150 steps per mile walking** and **1,450–1,550 steps per mile running** at an average height and pace. Divide your total marathon steps by 26.2 to get your own figure.

### Is walking a marathon the same as 56,000 steps a day?

On race day, yes. A walked marathon is the biggest single-day step total most people will ever record, more than five days of a 10,000-step goal. It isn't a sustainable daily target, so build up to it with long walks over several months.
